# Uptime research: Cloudflare incidents, SLAs and failure domains

Research slice for a Cloudflare-only Graftyard. Sources were read on 2026-10-26. Blog posts were fetched through the Wayback Machine (`web.archive.org/web/2026id_/<blog URL>`) because blog.cloudflare.com served a bot challenge to the fetcher. The URLs below are the original blog URLs. Scratch copies, the extraction output (`extract.json`, `arch.json`) and the status feed (`inc.json`) are in `/tmp/graftyard-cf/`. Every quoted sentence was checked by script to appear verbatim in the fetched source. [measured]

## Answer

Cloudflare's large outages are global, not regional, and most come from one change reaching every machine at once. The November 18, 2025 outage (about 3 h of core-traffic failure), the December 5, 2025 outage (25 min, 28% of HTTP traffic) and the July 14, 2025 1.1.1.1 outage (62 min) were config pushes through systems with no staged rollout. The June 12, 2025 outage (2 h 28 min) was a third-party storage dependency under Workers KV that also took down SQLite-backed Durable Objects, D1, Queues, Access and the dashboard. R2 had two global outages in 2025, both from single operator actions. On May 1, 2026 Cloudflare declared its Code Orange program complete and said config now rolls out progressively with health checks and "fail stale" defaults. No Cloudflare storage product I found states an RPO of zero across regions. R2 replicates within one region, Durable Objects do not move after creation, and D1 replicas are asynchronous. Only Workers (99.99%), R2 (99.9%) and Queues (99.9%) have service-specific SLAs, and the Workers SLA excludes failures of storage bindings.

## Findings

### A. Incidents with public post-mortems, 2023 to October 2026

Durations are as stated in the post unless marked derived. Cause classes follow the AWS slice: config push, deploy, dependency, power, capacity, other.

1. 2023-10-04, 1.1.1.1 resolver. 07:00 to 11:00 UTC. Dependency (new ZONEMD record in the root zone broke the parser, then a stale root zone's signatures expired). SERVFAIL "peaked at 15% of total queries", concentrated in Ashburn, Frankfurt and Singapore. Also hit WARP and Zero Trust through 1.1.1.1. Blast radius: global service, partial share of queries. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/1-1-1-1-lookup-failures-on-october-4th-2023/ [doc]
2. 2023-11-02 to 11-04, control plane and analytics. "The incident lasted from November 2 at 11:44 UTC until November 4 at 04:25 UTC." Power: a PGE transformer ground fault at Flexential PDX-04 also "shut down all of PDX-04's generators". The control plane runs "primarily on servers in three data centers around Hillsboro, Oregon", and "a subset of services that were supposed to be on the high availability cluster had dependencies on services exclusively running in PDX-04". Dashboard and API had intermittent errors, logs were lost, Stream uploads failed. "traffic through our network was not impacted." Blast radius: global control plane, data plane unaffected. The post's text does not name Workers, Durable Objects, R2, D1, KV or Queues. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/post-mortem-on-cloudflare-control-plane-and-analytics-outage/ [doc]
3. 2024-03-26, PDX01 power loss. Power: four Flexential switchboards failed together. "By 15:05 UTC, our APIs and Dashboards were operating normally, all without human intervention", seven minutes after the loss. Analytics "wasn't fully restored until later that day". Data center cold start took "roughly 10 hours" against "roughly 72 hours in November 2023". Blast radius: control plane and analytics only. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/major-data-center-power-failure-again-cloudflare-code-orange-tested/ [doc]
4. 2024-06-20, CDN latency and errors. 114 minutes. Config push: a new DDoS rule hit a latent infinite loop in a legacy Lua rate limiter, plus an unrelated backbone congestion event. "1.4 - 2.1% of HTTP requests to our CDN received a generic error page" at peak. "Impact from these events were observed in many Cloudflare data centers around the world." Global. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-incident-on-june-20-2024/ [doc]
5. 2024-06-27, 1.1.1.1 reachability. About 18:51 to 02:28 UTC (derived). Other (third-party BGP hijack and route leak). Unreachable "from over 300 networks in 70 countries", with a low share of users. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-1111-incident-on-june-27-2024/ [doc]
6. 2024-11-14, Cloudflare Logs. "roughly 3.5 hours", "about 55% of the logs we normally send to customers were not sent and were lost". Config push: "A bug in this system resulted in a blank configuration being provided to Logfwdr", which failed open and overloaded Buftee, whose overload protections were never configured. Global for Logs customers. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-incident-on-november-14-2024-resulting-in-lost-logs/ [doc]
7. 2025-02-06, R2. "unavailable for 59 minutes", 08:14 to 09:13 UTC with "a 100% error rate". Other (operator error): an abuse action "inadvertently disabled the R2 Gateway service instead of the specific endpoint/bucket". Also hit Stream, Images, Cache Reserve, Vectorize, log delivery, and "a 0.09% increase in error rates in calls to Durable Objects running in North America". Engineers tried to re-enable R2 "using our internal admin tooling, however this tooling was unavailable because it relies on R2." Global. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-incident-on-february-6-2025/ [doc]
8. 2025-03-21, R2. "1 hour and 7 minutes", 21:38 to 22:45 UTC. Deploy: new storage credentials went to a development Worker because the `--env` flag was omitted. "100% of write operations failed and approximately 35% of read operations to R2 failed globally." Images, Stream, Cache Reserve, Log Delivery and Vectorize were also hit. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-incident-march-21-2025/ [doc]
9. 2025-06-12, Workers KV and dependents. "This outage lasted 2 hours and 28 minutes, and globally impacted all Cloudflare customers using the affected services." Dependency: "Part of this infrastructure is backed by a third-party cloud provider, which experienced an outage today". KV failed about 90% of requests. Access failed 100% of identity logins. Dashboard logins failed. "SQLite-backed Durable Objects share the same underlying storage infrastructure as Workers KV", and DO and D1 error rates "peaked at 22%". "Queues message operations including–pushing and consuming–were unavailable during the incident window." Workers AI failed all inference. "DNS, Cache, proxy, WAF and related services were not directly impacted by this incident." Global. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-service-outage-june-12-2025/ [doc]
10. 2025-07-14, 1.1.1.1. 62 minutes, 21:52 to 22:54 UTC. Config push: a dormant topology error in a legacy addressing system caused "the global and immediate withdrawal of all 1.1.1.1 prefixes". "This was a global outage." The change "didn't go through a series of canary deployments before reaching every Cloudflare data center". Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-1-1-1-1-incident-on-july-14-2025/ [doc]
11. 2025-08-21, AWS us-east-1 interconnect. 16:27 to 20:18 UTC. Capacity: "a surge of traffic from a single customer that overloaded Cloudflare's links with AWS us-east-1." "This was a regional problem between Cloudflare and AWS us-east-1, and global Cloudflare services were not affected." Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-incident-on-august-21-2025/ [doc]
12. 2025-09-12, dashboard and API. 17:57 to 19:12 UTC (derived 75 min). Deploy: a dashboard React bug called the Tenant Service in a loop during a Tenant Service deploy. "When authorization evaluation fails, API requests return 5xx status codes." "The majority of users at Cloudflare were unaffected unless they were making configuration changes or using our dashboard." Global control plane, data plane unaffected. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/deep-dive-into-cloudflares-sept-12-dashboard-and-api-outage/ [doc]
13. 2025-11-18, global network. Start 11:20 UTC. "Core traffic was largely flowing as normal by 14:30." "As of 17:06 all systems at Cloudflare were functioning as normal." Config push: a ClickHouse permissions change doubled the Bot Management feature file, and "The larger-than-expected feature file was then propagated to all the machines that make up our network." The proxy panicked and returned 5xx. "This also affected Workers KV and Access, which rely on the core proxy." The dashboard failed through KV and Turnstile. Global. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage/ [doc]
14. 2025-12-05, FL1 proxy. "~25 minutes total impact", 08:47 to 09:12 UTC, "approximately 28% of all HTTP traffic served by Cloudflare". Config push: a WAF killswitch change for the React Server Components CVE went through a system that "does not perform gradual rollouts, but rather propagates changes within seconds to the entire fleet of servers in our network". It hit a nil-reference bug that "had existed undetected for many years". Global, subset of customers. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/5-december-2025-outage/ [doc]
15. 2026-01-08, 1.1.1.1. Release began 2026-01-07 23:48 UTC, revert completed 2026-01-08 19:55 UTC. Deploy: a memory optimization moved CNAME records after A records, which broke glibc `getaddrinfo` and some Cisco switches. "A global release containing the change starts" and later "The release reaches 90% of servers". Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cname-a-record-order-dns-standards/ [doc]
16. 2026-01-22, Miami route leak. "The route leak lasted 25 minutes". Config push: "an automated routing policy configuration error" leaked IPv6 prefixes from one Miami router. Regional. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/route-leak-incident-january-22-2026/ [doc]
17. 2026-02-20, BYOIP. "The total duration of the incident was 6 hours and 7 minutes". Deploy: a new cleanup sub-task called the Addressing API with an empty `pending_delete` value, which matched all BYOIP prefixes. "Out of the 4,306 total BYOIP prefixes, 25% of BYOIP prefixes were unintentionally withdrawn." "While this outage wasn't itself global, the blast radius and impact were unacceptably large". Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-outage-february-20-2026/ [doc]
18. The Post Mortem tag lists nothing newer than the May 1, 2026 Code Orange post. The Outage tag after that lists only Internet-disruption reports and the May 5, 2026 .de DNSSEC failure, which was DENIC's error, not Cloudflare's. Sources: https://blog.cloudflare.com/tag/post-mortem/ ; https://blog.cloudflare.com/tag/outage/ ; https://blog.cloudflare.com/de-tld-outage-dnssec/ [doc]

### C. Announced remediations

21. After November 2023, Cloudflare declared Code Orange. "We are shifting all non-critical engineering functions to focusing on ensuring high reliability of our control plane." The listed goals include "Ensure that the control plane running on the network continues to function even if all our core data centers are offline", tested DR plans for every GA product, and "Implement more rigorous chaos testing of all data center functions including the full removal of each of our core data center facilities". The March 2024 failover in finding 3 is the published test of this work. Source: finding 2 URL. [doc]
22. After the R2 events, Cloudflare added Admin API guardrails against disabling services in internal accounts, two-party approval for ad-hoc disablement, and key rotation only "through our hotfix release tooling instead of relying on manual wrangler command entry". Sources: finding 7 and 8 URLs. [doc]
23. After June 2025, KV moved storage in-house. "We are now storing all data on our own infrastructure." "We sharded data across multiple database clusters, each with three-way replication for durability and availability." It races writes to two backends and "returns success to the client as soon as the first backend confirms the write". The same post says "Our ultimate goal is to eliminate our remaining third-party storage dependency entirely", so it does not say the dependency is gone. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/rearchitecting-workers-kv-for-redundancy/ [doc]
24. After November and December 2025 Cloudflare declared Code Orange: Fail Small. Quicksilver changes reach "90% of servers on the network within seconds", and "in both incidents this caused a breaking change to propagate to the entire network in seconds rather than passing through gates to test it." The plan named health-mediated deployment for config, fail-open modules, and removal of circular dependencies in break-glass paths. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/fail-small-resilience-plan/ [doc]
25. On May 1, 2026 Cloudflare declared the work done. "In most cases, Cloudflare internal configuration changes no longer reach our network instantly and are instead rolled out progressively with real-time health monitoring." A new tool, Snapstone, applies health-mediated deployment to config. Systems "use the last known good configuration where possible ("fail stale")". Workers runtime changes ship by cohort, "starting with free customers first", so a bad change "would now only affect a small percentage of our free customers". Cloudflare built "backup authorization pathways for 18 key services". Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/code-orange-fail-small-complete/ [doc]
26. After February 2026, Cloudflare promised snapshot-based config with health-mediated rollout and a circuit breaker that halts "any out-of-control process that is manipulating the database". Source: finding 17 URL. [doc]

### D. SLAs

27. Workers (effective 2026-04-01): "Cloudflare will use commercially reasonable efforts to provide a Monthly Uptime Percentage of 99.99% for Customer's use of Workers". Errors are 5xx counted per 5-minute interval. Credits are 10% below 99.99%, 25% below 99.9% and 50% below 95%. The SLA excludes "the unavailability or performance degradation of interdependent Cloudflare services upon which the Worker relies (including but not limited to Cloudflare CDN, DNS, WAF, specialized storage bindings, etc.)". Source: https://www.cloudflare.com/workers-service-level-agreement/ [doc]
28. R2 (effective 2025-07-08) and Queues (effective 2026-04-01) each commit to "a Monthly Uptime Percentage of 99.9%", with credits of 10% below 99.9% and 25% below 99.0%. Sources: https://www.cloudflare.com/r2-service-level-agreement/ ; https://www.cloudflare.com/queues-service-level-agreement/ [doc]
29. The Enterprise SLA promises "The Service will serve Customer Content globally 100% of the time", and its Service-Specific SLA table lists only R2, Queues and Workers. The Business SLA promises "100% Uptime". I found no SLA for Durable Objects, D1 or KV. Sources: https://www.cloudflare.com/enterprise-support-sla/ ; https://www.cloudflare.com/business-sla/ [doc]

### E. Architecture facts that set failure domains

30. Quicksilver distributes all edge config. "Each data center must be able to successfully serve requests even if cut off from any source of central configuration or control." Cloudflare chose to "elect a single data center to aggregate writes". Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-quicksilver-configuration-distribution-at-internet-scale/ [doc] In v2, root nodes sit in "a few hyperscale core data centers that form our control plane", and replication is asynchronous so "we don't want a single server to slow down the entire tree". Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/quicksilver-v2-evolution-of-a-globally-distributed-key-value-store-part-1/ [doc]
31. Durable Object placement: "Durable Objects do not currently change locations after they are created". An object starts near the first `get()` or a best-effort location hint, and "Dynamic relocation of existing Durable Objects is planned for the future." Hints `sam`, `afr` and `me` spawn elsewhere. Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/ [doc]
32. Durable Object storage (SQLite backend): "Every time SQLite commits a transaction, SRS will immediately forward the change log to five "follower" machines across our network." A write is confirmed once "at least three of these followers respond". "Each of a database's five followers is located in a different physical data center." Changes reach object storage in batches "over a period of up to 10 seconds, or up to 16 MB". On host loss, a new instance starts only after a follower quorum is told to stop confirming writes for the old one. The post does not say whether followers span regions. Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/sqlite-in-durable-objects/ [doc]
33. Durable Object availability: "A Durable Object may be replaced in the event of a network partition or a software update". In-flight requests that touch storage after shutdown are "stopped immediately" with an error. Code ships "globally in an eventually consistent manner", so a new Worker can call an old DO version. Overloaded errors "should not be retried". Point-in-time recovery covers 30 days. Storage is 10 GB per object. Sources: https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/known-issues/ ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/ ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/error-handling/ ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/ [doc]
34. R2 internals: the R2 Gateway "is deployed across Cloudflare's global network via Cloudflare Workers". The Metadata Service is "built on Durable Objects". Object data is "replicated within the region (e.g. ENAM)", and 200 is sent only after the metadata commit. Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/how-r2-works/ [doc] R2 is "designed to provide 99.999999999% (eleven 9s) of annual durability" using clusters "distributed across several data centers within a geographic region", with synchronous writes. "Durability is not a guarantee of data availability." Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/durability/ [doc]
35. R2 location: Automatic picks "the closest available region". Hints are "a best effort and not a guarantee". Jurisdictions are `eu`, `fedramp` and `us`. "Once an R2 bucket is created, the jurisdiction cannot be changed." Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/ [doc] Local Uploads writes data "to storage near the client, then asynchronously replicated to your bucket". Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/local-uploads/ [doc]
36. R2 consistency: reads after writes, deletes and lists are strongly consistent. Concurrent `If-None-Match: *` writes "are serialized", and at most one succeeds while the rest get 412. A 404 does not prove an earlier in-flight PUT failed. Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/ [doc]
37. D1 has one primary in "one location in the world". Read replicas are asynchronous, so "a read replica may be arbitrarily out of date", and "All write queries are still forwarded to the primary database instance." Time Travel restores to any minute in 30 days. Sources: https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/read-replication/ ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/time-travel/ [doc]
38. KV "stores data in a small number of centralized data centers" and changes "may take up to 60 seconds or more to be visible in other global network locations". Source: https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/concepts/how-kv-works/ [doc]
39. Jurisdictions on DO and D1 limit where data runs and persists, not who can call it. "Workers may still access Durable Objects constrained to a jurisdiction from anywhere in the world." DO jurisdictions are `eu`, `us` and `fedramp`. D1 jurisdictions are set only at creation. Sources: finding 31 URL ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/configuration/data-location/ [doc]
40. Cross-region RPO zero: R2 (finding 34), D1 (finding 37) and KV (finding 23) are documented as in-region or asynchronous across regions, and Durable Objects (finding 32) do not state whether followers cross regions. I found no Cloudflare page that states a cross-region RPO of zero. [inference]

### F. Cloudflare Basin

41. Basin exists. It went GA on 2026-10-01 as the new name of the Cloudflare Data Platform. "Basin, formerly the Cloudflare Data Platform, is now generally available." Basin Pipelines was Cloudflare Pipelines, Basin Catalog was R2 Data Catalog, and Basin SQL was R2 SQL. It is an analytics platform over Apache Iceberg tables in R2, not a transactional store. Sources: https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-10-01-basin-ga/ ; https://developers.cloudflare.com/basin/ [doc]

## Implications for the design

Each point compares with the AWS findings in `research/uptime.md`. All items are [inference].

1. Blast radius is global on Cloudflare, not regional. AWS's worst event stayed in us-east-1 (AWS finding 32), and AWS deploys zonally (AWS finding 4). Cloudflare's worst events reached every machine within seconds (findings 10, 13, 14). Code Orange (finding 25) says this is fixed for config, but it was declared complete in May 2026 and has five months of history. Graftyard cannot get regional isolation from the platform. Isolation has to come from Graftyard's own cohorts and from Workers' cohort ordering, which ships free accounts first.
2. Treat KV as a control-plane dependency and keep it off the commit path. AWS's DynamoDB failure cascaded through EC2 (AWS finding 33). Cloudflare's KV storage failure took down DO, D1, Queues, Access and the dashboard together (finding 9), and KV is eventually consistent at 60 s (finding 38). Push, fetch and ref reads must not need KV, Access or the dashboard.
3. Every Cloudflare storage product shares lower layers. R2 metadata runs on Durable Objects, the R2 gateway is a Worker (finding 34), and DO and D1 shared KV's storage in June 2025 (finding 9). Holding refs in DO and packs in R2 is two products with one failure domain, not two independent domains.
4. A per-repository chain fits Durable Objects well. One DO per repository serializes commits, a commit is confirmed by 3 of 5 followers in separate buildings (finding 32), and output gates hold the response until the write is durable. This is the counterpart of DSQL or DynamoDB MRSC in the AWS design, but a weaker one. The DO does not relocate (finding 31), and the 13-day status sample had 7 DO incidents, most of them regional (finding 20).
5. RPO zero across regions is not available off the shelf. AWS offers it in DSQL and MRSC (AWS findings 26 and 29). On Cloudflare, AWS implication 12 (an acknowledged push survives loss of a region) holds only if Graftyard writes synchronously to two DOs in different location hints, or to two R2 buckets in different regions, before it acks. The SLO must say so if the design does not do this.
6. R2 conditional writes can be the CAS for the chain head. `If-None-Match: *` is serialized (finding 36), the same contract as S3 (AWS finding 31). The ambiguous-outcome rule means a retry must use the same conditional write, never a read-then-decide.
7. Plan for single-region loss of R2 data. R2 data lives in one region (findings 34, 35), and AWS lost ME-SOUTH-1 data for good (AWS finding 38). Pack durability across regions needs two buckets with different location hints, written before the ack. Jurisdiction choice is permanent per bucket.
8. Operator error is a first-class cause. Both 2025 R2 outages came from one manual action (findings 7, 8). Graftyard's own key rotation, bucket config and DO namespace changes need the same release tooling and two-person rule Cloudflare adopted (finding 22).
9. The control plane can fail while the data plane runs. This matches AWS static stability (AWS finding 1). Cloudflare's dashboard and API failed in 2023, 2024 and 2025 while traffic flowed (findings 2, 3, 12). Graftyard's runtime must not call the Cloudflare API, and Workers deploys are not a recovery tool during an incident.
10. The SLA stack is thinner than AWS's. Workers is 99.99% but excludes storage bindings. R2 and Queues are 99.9%. DO, D1 and KV have none (findings 27 to 29). AWS DSQL multi-region carries 99.999% (AWS finding 26). A Graftyard SLO above 99.9% for git writes rests on DO, which has no contractual number.
11. Fail stale is the shared lesson. AWS pushes credentials ahead of need (AWS finding 6), and Cloudflare now keeps the last known good config (finding 25). Graftyard should cache auth decisions and repository routing so a KV or Access outage (findings 9, 13) does not stop git reads.
12. Code versions are mixed during deploys. Workers and DOs update "in an eventually consistent manner" (finding 33), so the Worker-to-DO protocol must accept N-1 in both directions. This is the same rule as AWS implication 24, applied to every deploy.

## Open questions

1. Do the five SQLite followers of a Durable Object span more than one region? The blog says "different physical data center", not region. A confirmed write's survival of a regional loss depends on the answer.
2. Where does a Durable Object restart after its host data center fails, and how long does the follower fencing take? I found no stated RTO.
3. Does SQLite-backed DO storage still depend on a third-party provider? The KV post says KV data is in-house but that a "remaining third-party storage dependency" exists, and June 2025 showed DO shares KV's storage.
4. The status sample covers 13 days. I could not page cloudflarestatus.com history for 2023 to 2026, so incident counts for DO, R2 and D1 over longer periods are unverified.
5. The Code Orange claims (Snapstone, cohort deploys, fail stale) are Cloudflare's own and are five months old. No outage since May 2026 has tested them in public.
6. No blog post-mortem covers June to October 2026. The October 23, 2026 "Network connectivity issues affecting services in North America" (major, 15:47 to 18:43 UTC per the status feed) has no published root cause. [measured]
