# M0-WP11: answers from research and measurement (2026-10-27)

The operator does not expect a written answer from Cloudflare ("assume i won't hear from cloudflare on number 2. investigate and try to find the answers yourself"). Each answer below says what was measured, what the documents say, and what remains open.

## (a) Does a Durable Object call through a binding avoid the front-line proxy?

**Answer: almost certainly yes for the request itself. The internal transport is still not documented.** [measured, doc, inference]

- Measured (`graft-m0-tools`, `/route`): the edge Worker's own public request arrived with `cf-ray`, `cf-connecting-ip`, `cf-visitor`, `cf-ipcountry`, `x-forwarded-proto`, and `x-real-ip`. The same Worker's `stub.fetch()` reached a `RouteProbe` in MIA, MRS, and SIN carrying only the one header the caller set (`x-m0`), with `request.cf` null. A request that crossed FL would carry FL's headers and a `cf` object. The binding round trips were 63 to 67 ms from LAX to MIA and 149 to 152 ms from LAX to MRS, with no TLS or proxy overhead visible beyond the distance.
- Docs: a service binding calls another Worker "without going through a publicly-accessible URL", and "Isolate services from the public Internet" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/service-bindings/). The November 18, 2025 postmortem lists every impacted service. Workers KV appears there because "requests to KV's 'front end' gateway failed due to the core proxy failing". Durable Objects and the Workers runtime are absent from that list. The December 5, 2025 postmortem limits impact to "Customers that have their web assets served by our older FL1 proxy AND had the Cloudflare Managed Ruleset deployed" (https://blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage, https://blog.cloudflare.com/5-december-2025-outage).
- Still open: the transport between two colos. It is internal to Cloudflare and is not FL as a zone sees it. It shares Cloudflare's backbone and object routing, which the design already counts as shared by all three sites.

## (b) Do a Durable Object's storage followers span regions?

**Answer: no. The confirming quorum sits in the object's own region.** [measured, doc]

- Docs: "Each of a database's five followers is located in a different physical data center... it is always easy for us to find four other data centers nearby any Durable Object (in addition to the one it is running in)." A write is confirmed once "at least three of these followers respond" (https://blog.cloudflare.com/sqlite-in-durable-objects).
- Measured (`run/wp11-quorum.ts`, `results/wp11-quorum.json`): ten objects per hint, 19 writes each, timing `ctx.storage.sync()` after each write. The output-gate reading, an outgoing fetch held until the write is confirmed, agrees with `sync()` within 1 ms.

| Hint | Colos | Fastest object, median ms | Median object, ms | Slowest object, ms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| enam | IAD, ORD, EWR, ATL, MIA | 15 | 20 | 30 |
| wnam | LAX, SJC, DFW, DEN | 10 | 30 | 35 |
| weur | AMS, LHR, MAD, CDG | 7 | 9 | 21 |
| eeur | OTP, PRG, ARN, FRA, VIE, WAW | 14 | 19 | 31 |
| apac | SIN, NRT, KIX, HKG | 2 | 8 | 37 |
| oc | MEL, SYD, BNE | 13 | 13 | 16 |

- The slowest object confirms in 37 ms. The shortest cross-region member round trip M0-WP1 measured was 73 ms (enam to weur, `results/b0-12.json`, `a-b` p0). A write confirmed in under 37 ms cannot have reached a follower that is 73 ms away, so the three followers that confirmed it are all closer than any other `Tri` site, which means they are in the object's own region. The fifth follower could be anywhere, but no confirmed write waits for it.
- Consequence: a confirmed write in a `Solo` or `Alone` member survives a data-center loss and is not guaranteed to survive a region loss. Flip condition 2 does not fire.

## (c) Where does a Durable Object restart after a region failure, and can old and new instances run together?

**Answer: the documents rule out two instances confirming writes. They do not say where an object restarts after a whole region fails.** [doc, inference]

- Fencing: "if we can't reach the DO's host, we can instead try to contact its followers. If we can contact at least three of the five followers, and tell them to stop confirming writes for the unreachable DO instance, then we know that instance is unable to confirm any more writes going forward. We can then safely start up a new instance to replace the unreachable one" (the SQLite blog above).
- Old instance still running: "Uniqueness is enforced upon starting a new event... and upon accessing storage... if the event takes some time to execute and does not ever access its durable storage, then it is possible that the Durable Object may no longer be current... If the event accesses storage at this point, it will receive an exception" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/known-issues/). An old leader can therefore still answer a call that touches no storage, but it cannot confirm a write. Graftyard's leader writes storage for every log entry, so a stale leader cannot commit.
- Restart location: a replacement starts only after 3 of 5 followers agree to stop the old instance. Those followers sit in the same region as the object, per (b). A whole-region loss takes out that quorum, so the object cannot restart anywhere until its region returns [inference]. "Durable Objects do not currently change locations after they are created" and "Dynamic relocation of existing Durable Objects is planned for the future" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/) agree with that.
- Consequence: `Solo`'s RTO for a region loss is the region's own outage, and its RPO is the writes the region had not yet uploaded to object storage, which is batched "every 10 seconds" or 16 MB. `Tri` does not depend on this answer.

## (d) Do Artifacts forks share objects with their source?

**Answer: no. Each fork is billed and capped at the source's full size.** [measured]

- Method: one 200,888,320-byte repository of 8 commits of 25 MB of random data, forked four times through `POST .../repos/src/fork`. Size came from the Analytics GraphQL dataset `artifactsStorageAdaptiveGroups`, `max.repositorySizeBytes`, per repository.
- Result: at 22:15Z and 22:30Z, `src` and `fork-1` to `fork-3` each reported 200,888,320 bytes, and every fork returned `objects: 24`, the source's full object count. Pricing says "Storage is calculated across all repositories", so four forks bill as five copies. The fourth fork took 8.4 s to return for 191 MiB.
- Also measured: one push over 64 MiB was refused with `artifacts_git_receive_pack_request_too_large: push is 190.8 MiB, limit is 64.0 MiB`. No page read stated that limit.
- The repositories and the `graft-m0-wp11` namespace were deleted afterwards.
- Consequence: the Artifacts flip condition, which needs forks that share objects, fails. Artifacts keeps no role (A12).
