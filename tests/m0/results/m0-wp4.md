# M0-WP4: site days, verify refusals, and drain (2026-10-28)

The prototype is `src/raft/` with the M0-WP4 additions (`results/m0-wp4-plan.md`). It runs as `graft-wp4-a/b/c` and `graft-wp4-edge`, a separate copy of the M0-WP2 scripts with its own fault plan (`PLAN` "wp4"), so B0-11's 48-hour run on `graft-raft-*` is not touched. `run/raft.ts deploy` refuses to deploy `graft-raft-*` unless told to. Raw runs are in `results/raw/site-day-*.json`.

What the prototype builds, and what it leaves out:

- **Release variables.** Each member version carries `RELEASE`, `READS` (the highest entry `format` it decodes), `WRITES` (the format its leaders write for the rare key kind `new:`), and `BREAK_KIND` (`apply:<prefix>` throws while applying such an event; `refuse` refuses every events entry in `verify`).
- **Refusal rules** (`design/DESIGN.md`, `verify`). A refusal carries the refuser's release and index. The leader classifies it after the refused entry's round settles: older refuser and uncommitted entry means truncate, persist `no_lead_until`, and transfer; both own-release followers refusing means own defect, truncate, and alarm; anything else means keep leading, add the refuser to `cannot_follow`, and alarm. `no_lead_until` clears at boot when the release changes, and `cannot_follow` clears when the refuser reports another release.
- **`Release` group.** One Raft group named `release` on the same three sites, holding `SiteMode` as register keys. The drain guard is in `decideMode` (`src/raft/types.ts`). Members read the modes from the group's applied state, cached per isolate for `POINTER_TTL` (5 s), never moving backward. The `release` group drains like any other group.
- **Drain.** A leader on a drained, lead-limited, or `no_lead_until` site hands off on its next request (checked at most once a second, since groups are request-driven) to a follower that may lead. Such a member refuses `TimeoutNow` and campaigns on its own only when pre-vote finds no voter that can lead.
- **Site day.** `run/site-day.ts` stands in for `graft-ops`: drain, `wrangler versions upload`, `wrangler versions deploy` at 1%, 10%, 50%, and 100% with `--durable-objects-code-update-mode immediate`, gates from the members' own counters, rollback by deploying N at 100%, undrain under the 1% lead limit, and a first day. Load is 100 writes/s spread over every group.
- **`SiteHealth`.** One object per (observer, target) on the observer's site, fed by members' AppendEntries and by edge calls labelled with an observer site.
- **Not built.** Signatures and keys, `ReleaseUploaded`, the pre-signed `undo` as a signed entry (the runner holds the rollback), `Pointers`, and spares (Milestone 5). Dwell is seconds, not `STEP_DWELL`'s 2 h, and `STEP_MIN_ENTRIES` is 20, not 1,000.

## Slow writes: two election stalls on idle groups

Every 1,000-group run, including controls with no drain and no deploy, had a handful of writes take 9 to 13 s (`site-day-ctl-1.json`: 5 of 42,000 over one lease; `site-day-ctl-2.json`: 2 of 24,000). The edge traced each slow write (`slowTraces`). Every one had the same shape: an idle group whose leader had been evicted (a member restarts on its next request, and status then shows it a follower holding `led_term`), the old leader answered `NotLeader` with no leader named after a follower started a higher term, the other two members refused each other's pre-vote ("promised", "contending", "log behind"), and the edge reported `suspect` only to the two followers, so nobody won until the promises lapsed, 10 to 12 s later.

The prototype refused every candidate while a promise ran, but the design's promise bars votes only for "a rival" (`MemberState.promise_until`). Fixed in the prototype and stated in the design (`suspect` doc comment): a promise does not bar the leader it was given to, a reset leader still in `led_term` resumes it rather than campaign, and the edge also reports to a member that answered `NotLeader` with no leader named. Local chaos with Porcupine on the new code: Ok on 5,620 operations, 0 failed (`raft-chaos-local-wp4-promise-1.json`). The rerun control on Cloudflare: 30,000 writes, 0 failed, 0 over one lease, max 6.6 s, p99 1,557 ms (`site-day-ctl-3.json`). The p99 over 1 s on 1,000 groups is the load: 100 writes/s spread over 1,000 groups means each write usually finds a cold group, whose first request restarts its objects.

A second cause showed up while draining a site that led 977 groups (`site-day-drain-a1.json`: 5 writes at 11 to 15 s, 0 failed). Pre-vote refused nearly every candidate as "quarantine": the flapping rule refuses a member with two boots in 5 minutes, and an idle group's objects are evicted and restarted on their next request every minute or so, so every member of a request-driven group looked like it was flapping. Fixed in the design (`MemberState.boots`) and the prototype: a boot counts only if it comes within 5 s of the member's last saved state, so an idle eviction is not a reset. The first cut used 30 s, which still counted evictions: idle members are evicted after about 10 s [measured: of six groups idle 2 to 12 s, members restarted at 10 s and 12 s and not before]. Local chaos on the new code: Porcupine Ok on 5,578 operations, 0 failed (`raft-chaos-local-wp4-boots-1.json`). The same drain of A on Cloudflare: 14,000 writes, 0 failed, none over one lease, max 3.3 s (`site-day-drain-a2.json`).

Both changes are in `graft-wp4-*` only. `graft-raft-*` keeps the code under B0-11's 48-hour test, so that run does not cover them; a short Cloudflare chaos run on `graft-wp4-*` should before Milestone 0 closes. (Run on 2026-10-30; see "Chaos run on graft-wp4". The claim above that the p99 over 1 s "is the load" of cold groups did not hold up; see "B0-04: what the p99 measures".)

## B0-07: why groups on C moved late (2026-10-29)

**Root cause, from the slow traces of runs 6 to 8.** 20 groups led on C have their hand-off in the members' logs (`slowTraces`). C's leader logged its first "handing off (drained)" 4.7 to 35.6 s after the drain committed, median 14.3 s, and 13 of the 20 had at least one hand-off "abandoned". So F34's hypothesis holds, and it has two parts, plus a third found on the first rerun:

1. **The leader learned of the drain late.** `maybeHandOff` ran only on a `commit` or `read` the leader received, and half the edge's calls to C hung. The isolate's `Release` view was then up to `POINTER_TTL` old. A refresh that every member failed marked the view fresh for another 5 s. The `release` followers applied a committed `SiteMode` only when the next `release` entry came, so a refresh from C reached the drain only through the `release` leader on A, over a link that hung half the time.
2. **Hand-offs on the gray link were abandoned.** `replicate` gave up on a follower after `COMMIT_RETRIES` failures once no round was waiting, but `transfer` waits only for `match[to]`, so it waited out the whole `LEASE` and abandoned. Three lost `TimeoutNow` calls in a row also abandoned it.
3. **A transfer could depose the leader and leave none** (run 9, group `-35`, 57.9 s). The target won `TimeoutNow` but its vote was refused "promised" by B, which had not yet seen the transfer id, and its vote to the old leader on C hung. Its failed campaign still raised the term, so C stepped down and the group had no leader until its next write 40 s later.

**Fixes** (`src/raft/member.ts`, all in `graft-wp4-*` only):

- **Drain alarm.** A leader arms a Durable Object alarm 2 s after it serves a `commit` or `read` (`DRAIN_CHECK`). The alarm checks the site mode, waiting for a stale view's refresh, hands off if the leader must not lead, and re-arms while the group served a request or committed an entry within `DRAIN_IDLE` (60 s). An evicted leader still holding `led_term` resumes only to hand off. After `DRAIN_IDLE` no alarm is set, so an idle group is not woken; it moves on its next request, as before. Campaigns stay request-driven.
- **View refresh.** A refresh that gets no newer view retries in 1 s, not a full `POINTER_TTL`. The `release` leader sends each commit to its followers at once, so every `release` member's applied state carries a committed `SiteMode` [inference: this was not measured on its own].
- **Transfer.** `replicate` keeps retrying the transfer target while a transfer runs. The leader waits up to 2 x `CALL_DEADLINE` for the other follower to ack an AppendEntries carrying the transfer id, and repeats `TimeoutNow` until the transfer's `LEASE` ends.
- **Checks before Cloudflare.** On the local bed (`.gen/wp4-local.jsonc`), four groups each wrote once and then went idle. Draining their leader's site 1.5 s later moved all four in 4.3 to 4.9 s with no request, and the leader logged "handing off (drained)" from the alarm. Local chaos with Porcupine after each protocol change: Ok on 29,737, 29,961, and 29,676 operations, 0 failed (`raft-chaos-local-wp4-drainalarm-1.json` to `-3.json`; the last one is the final code).
- **Runner.** The 1-second poll reads only A's and B's members, because status calls to C hang half the time, which stretched each poll to about 3 s. A member there leading, or holding `led_term` for the highest term any answering member reports, counts as moved. The runner also records move time against each group's idle time before the drain (`moveByIdle`), and reads the members' logs for any group over 20 s (`late`).

## B0-04: what the p99 measures (2026-10-29)

The runner now writes every operation to `results/raw/site-day-<run>.ops.jsonl` and splits latency by how long the group had been idle and by the site that leads the group at the end (`latencyByIdleGap`, `latencyByLeader`). It also reads `SiteHealth` per second (`healthSeries`) before it stops the load, because an idle `SiteHealth` object is evicted with its in-memory counts. Run b0-04-9's series read empty for that reason. The member log now records how long a failed append waited, and any storage confirmation over 500 ms.

| b0-04-10 | the same | 36,000 | 638 ms | 1,062 / 524 / 435 ms | A 638 ms | 0 |

What this shows, and what it rules out:

- **Cold groups do not set the p99.** A write to a group idle for 20 s or more, whose members the platform has evicted (about 10 s, above), had the same p99 as a write to a group used within 2 s. Only each group's first write of the run is slower (p50 about 530 ms against 190 ms), which is 2.5 to 4% of writes.
- **Leader placement sets it.** The load runs from one colo (LAX, through the edge in the same colo). Groups led on C (apac) had p99 1,552 ms and groups led on A (enam) 741 ms in the same run. The old controls with p99 1,005 to 1,557 ms had 27 to 160 groups led on C and 332 to 725 on B. The reruns had every group led on A, because the k1 set's leaders had collected there, so B's drain moved 0 and 1 groups. They therefore test the deploy restarts at each step (543 to 613 of B's members on N+1 at 100%) but not a drain that moves many leaders under load. B-led groups were not measured separately.
- **The b0-04-8 stall was not caused by the 50% step's restarts.** By the members' clocks against the deployment's `created_on`, A's appends to both B and C began missing `CALL_DEADLINE` 5.4 s before Cloudflare recorded the 50% deployment, while the runner was still judging the 10% step. 15 traced groups led on A saw it, and A's calls to B and C kept failing together from 108 s to 139 s into the day. A fault on A's side is the likeliest shape, but it is unproven.
- **Ruled out: abandoned calls holding connections.** One member started 0, 3, 6, or 8 calls to B that hung past `CALL_DEADLINE` (`/connprobe`, `hold`), then pinged C. The pings took 202 to 213 ms in every case, so a member's calls abandoned at the deadline do not delay its calls to the other follower.
- **Not reproduced.** In b0-04-10 the per-second health series show at most 3 missed calls a second from A to B or C across the whole day (137 of 82,579 calls), with no burst at any step. The stall did not recur in two reruns.
- **Caveat on b0-04-10.** 40 of the runner's `/bench` calls failed at the orb's own `fetch` ("TypeError: fetch failed", two bursts of 20). Their up to 4,000 writes are not in the history, so their outcome is unknown; the 0 failed is over the 36,000 recorded.

## Bugs found and fixed while building

- **A lost `TimeoutNow` abandoned a whole transfer.** On a gray link, drain hand-offs were often "abandoned (lost)". `TimeoutNow` now retries `COMMIT_RETRIES` times like any commit-path call. A repeat is safe, because the target answers "leader" once it has won.
- **A leader stopped probing a follower it marked unable to follow.** The 5 s probe returned from the replication loop, and nothing restarted it while the follower was behind, so a leader never learned that C had rolled forward and kept C in `cannot_follow`, which then breached the next site day's gate (`site-day-b0-07-1.json`). Fixed: the loop waits out the 5 s instead of stopping, and a refusing follower's `NotLeader`-style replies carry its release. After C was rolled forward, both sampled leaders cleared C within one write. The runner also gates only `cannot_follow` marks that are new since the day started.

- **A drained candidate and a voter that is behind livelocked the `release` group.** Pre-vote denied every drained candidate with "voter can lead" when the voter could lead, even when its own log was behind and it could never win. Each side then lost. Fixed: a voter defers a drained candidate only if its log is at least as current. Found on the `release` group after a redeploy reset all three members.
- **A `STEP_DOWN` or drain transfer could pick a drained or unable-to-follow target.** The target now must be allowed to lead, and a drained member refuses `TimeoutNow`.
- **The site-day runner's 1% step judged members still on N.** `versions deploy` defaults to a deferred Durable Object code update of up to 5 minutes (wrangler 4.x release notes, `--durable-objects-code-update-mode`), so the first runs saw 0 to 2 N+1 members at 1% and halted on too few entries. With `immediate`, a 10% deployment put 29 of 300 site-C members on N+1 within 5 s, and 1% put 1 of 300 [measured].
- **A hand-off waited out the whole lease after its catch-up gave up** (B0-07, 2026-10-29). `replicate` stopped retrying a failing follower once no round waited, while `transfer` waited only for that follower's `match`. Now the loop keeps going while the follower is the transfer target, `TimeoutNow` repeats until the transfer's `LEASE` ends, and the leader waits up to 2 x `CALL_DEADLINE` for the other follower to ack the transfer id, so the target's vote is not refused "promised" and does not leave the group without a leader.
- **`site-day.ts day --control` crashed in `finish`** on a temporal-dead-zone read of `autoDrain` (`site-day-ctl-4.out`). Fixed by declaring it before the control branch returns.

## Proposed design edits

I made none of these; each needs the owner's edit and the checks in `AGENTS.md`.

1. **`design/DESIGN.md`, Shape, `SiteMode` doc comment.** Sentence now: "Members on a drained site hand off leadership within one lease and keep voting and storing." Proposed: "A leader on a drained site checks its site's mode from a Durable Object alarm every 2 s while its group served a request or committed an entry within the last 60 s, and on every request, and hands off within one lease. A group idle longer is not woken; its leader hands off on its next request. Members on a drained site keep voting and storing." Evidence: "B0-07: why groups on C moved late" above. With request-only checks, groups led on C moved at p50 14.6 to 20.8 s and max 55 to 68 s after the drain (runs 6 to 8). With the alarm, active groups moved at p50 5.4 to 6.3 s, and 229 of 230 groups that wrote within 50 s before the drain moved within 20 s (runs 11 to 13). The constants are prototype values [guess].

## STEP_DOWN's 3-slow-confirmations rule and B0-06 (2026-10-30)

The design now trips `STEP_DOWN` on confirm latency only when at least 3 storage confirmations in the 10 s window pass `confirm_p99_ms` (500 ms) (`design/DESIGN.md`, Shape, the `Health` doc comment). The commit-error half is unchanged. Site B was first reset to release 1 (`site-day.ts reset --site b`, version `164347b5`), since b0-07-13 had left it on release 2.

**What changed in the prototype** (`graft-wp4-*` only, deployed 02:09Z and 02:29Z):

- **The rule** (`src/raft/member.ts`, `checkHealth`; `STEP_DOWN` in `src/raft/types.ts` gains `confirmSlowMin: 3`). The confirm half trips when p99 > 500 ms and at least 3 confirmations in the window are over 500 ms. The old minimum of 20 samples before p99 counted is gone. ## F35: slow confirmations on healthy leaders (2026-10-30)

F35 (`decisions/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`) asked why leaders on healthy site B saw storage confirmations of 509 to 1,018 ms while A's members reset under B0-06's failing-write fault, enough for the 3-write `STEP_DOWN` rule to move them (writes2 and writes3 above).

### Cause: the object's own storage, not the member

**Instrumented member** (deployed to `graft-wp4-*` at 03:33Z for the diagnosis runs, removed from the final code except one log line). Four failing-write runs and two no-fault runs of 2 minutes (`b0-06-writes-f35-diag1.json` to `-diag4`, `b0-06-none-f35-diagnone.json`, `-diagnone2.json`):

- **Not the event loop.** In every one of the 29 leader and 52 follower confirmations over 500 ms, the ticker was never more than 20 ms late ("loop lag max 0 ms"), so the isolate kept running while the write waited. ### Fix, part 2: a stepped-down member is not a `STEP_DOWN` target

**What slow3 and slow-r10 showed.** A's leader tripped on confirm latency and moved to B correctly. Then B and C passed the group back and forth on **commit errors**, one change every 8 to 20 s: "commit errors 0.39/0.07, c>a fails 0.00, transferring to c", then "commit errors 0.44/0.08, b>a fails 0.00, transferring to b", and so on. The rate to A was high because A was the faulty site. The rate on the healthy B-C link was just over the 5% threshold (0.06 to 0.10), and the per-follower rule moves a leader only when every follower's rate passes it. Of 97 deadline misses on healthy links across this section's runs, 70 had a follower-confirm stall on the callee (the output gate holding its reply) and 26 a confirm stall on the caller, 89 of 97 either. Pre-vote already refuses such a member as a campaigner (design `RESET_QUARANTINE`: "Pre-vote and transfers refuse a candidate whose two `boots`, or whose `stepped_down_at`, fall inside it"). The prototype applied that to pre-vote but not to the `STEP_DOWN` target, so this matches the design's stated rule rather than adding one. A real sick leader on a healthy pair still moves once. It can move back only after 5 minutes, which caps a pair's ping-pong at 2 changes in 5 minutes. Local Porcupine on this code: Ok on 29,845 operations, 0 failed (`raft-chaos-local-wp4-f35-2.json`). Deployed to `graft-wp4-*` at 05:38Z. `graft-raft-*`, `graft-m0-tools`, and `graft-m0-chaos` were not touched, and fault plan "plan" was not set.

## Audit: fault-plan reader (2026-10-30)

This audit was read-only: no deploys, and no calls to `graft-raft-*`, `graft-m0-tools`, or `graft-m0-chaos` that change state. B0-11's 48-hour run is still going on `graft-raft-*`.

### When an isolate keeps an old plan

`Faults.current()` in `src/faults.ts` (the same in `results/raw/cf-48h-src/faults.ts` except the plan name) starts a refresh only when `refreshing` is null and the cached plan is over 1 s old. The `.finally` that clears `refreshing` and moves `fetchedAt` runs only if the `get()` promise settles. If it never settles, `refreshing` stays set for the rest of the isolate's life, and no later refresh starts. Two cases follow:

- **(a) Stuck after one good read.** The isolate keeps that plan until it is evicted. While the plan is constant, nothing changes. When the plan changes, a new fault never starts there (lighter), or an old one never stops (heavier).
- **(b) Stuck on the isolate's first read** (`fetchedAt === 0`). The roll that started the read awaits it forever. In a member this is `faultyStub`, so that one call hangs. Every later roll sees `EMPTY`, because `rollNow` and `cutAt` read `this.plan`, which is still `EMPTY`. That isolate injects nothing until it is evicted.

**Why the read would never settle** is not established. It was observed once, in `wp4-cf-4` ("Two-hour chaos run" above): site C's members saw none of three later plan changes for 9+ minutes, while A and B saw all of them. The likeliest trigger is the calling object being reset (`ctx.abort`) while its `FaultControl.get()` call is in flight. A reset ends that object's pending I/O, but the `Faults` object lives at module scope and outlives it, so the isolate keeps a promise that may never settle [inference]. Resets are what chaos and B0-26 do, so the runs most exposed are the ones with many resets and a plan that changes.

**How long:** until the isolate is evicted, which an idle isolate is in about 10 s to a few minutes. A busy one may last the whole run. `wp4-cf-4` shows at least 9 minutes on one site.

**Read-only evidence available:** no run before `wp4-cf-5` recorded `Faults.stats` (per-isolate counts of rolls and injected faults); no route returns it. What does survive is the members' logs: "append to X failed: InjectedFault …" lines name the caller and callee, and `STEP_DOWN` lines give reasons. Those are in some raw files (`b0-06-*.json` from M0-WP4, `site-day-*.json` traces) and not in others.

## Leader kill: the follow-on `STEP_DOWN` (2026-10-30)

M0-WP12 saw a second leader change 20 to 24 s after a leader kill in 5 of 7 runs (`results/m0-wp12.md`, second pass, item 2 and edit 10). Here it is reproduced on `graft-wp4-*`, traced to its cause, and fixed.

**Runner** (`run/raft-kill.ts`, new). 30 groups led on A take 100 writes/s in total from 20 callers. At the kill, every group's member on A is reset (`/reset`, `ctx.abort`), and plan "wp4" fails every call to and from A from 2 s before the kill until 15 s after it, as M0-WP12 did. The runner samples every group's leader each second for 60 s, then reads each member's log. A run's file is `results/raw/kill-<run>.json`, and the runner will not overwrite a finished one, because a supervised service reruns its command when it exits.

**Instrumentation** (`src/raft/member.ts`, kept). A `STEP_DOWN` on commit errors now logs, per follower, the calls in the window, the failures by kind (`deadline`, `injected`, `other`), and how far that follower is behind the leader's last entry.

**Cause, from the members' logs** (`kill-pre1`, before the fix; `kill-pre2` repeats it). In `kill-pre1`, 3 of 30 groups changed leader twice in the 60 s after the kill (`-g22`, `-g23`, `-g25`). In all three, the new leader B tripped `STEP_DOWN` 23.2 to 23.8 s after the kill, about 10 s after it won (13.4 to 14.8 s). B's first acknowledged call to A came at 15.3 to 16.1 s, after every failed one. The logged reasons:

| Group | New leader won | `STEP_DOWN` | Errors to A (the killed member) | Errors to C (healthy) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-g22` | 13.4 s | 23.8 s, "commit errors 0.37/0.10" | 7 of 19 calls, all `injected` | 1 of 10, `deadline` |
| `-g23` | 14.8 s | 23.4 s, "commit errors 0.11/0.30" | 3 of 27, all `injected` | 3 of 10, `deadline` |
| `-g25` | 13.6 s | 23.2 s, "commit errors 0.33/0.20" | 8 of 24, all `injected` | 2 of 10, `deadline` |

So there are two parts:

1. **Calls to the killed member.** B wins while A's site is still failing (the fault runs to 15 s). Its first AppendEntries to A fail before A has ever answered it in this term. In `-g23`, B logged "append to a ok after 3 failures" at 15.3 s, its first contact with A. Those failures stay in the 10 s window until about 24 s and put A's rate over 5%.
2. **A far follower judged on its 10th call.** C is apac, B is weur, and their link misses `CALL_DEADLINE` (1 s) about once in 10 calls even with no fault: in `-g22`, C as the later leader missed a deadline to B every 2 to 3 s for the rest of the run. Under this load C gets about 1 call a second, and `checkHealth` judges a follower only once it has 10 calls in the window. So C's rate first counts about 10 s after the election, and a single miss is 0.10, over 5%.

Both together trip the rule, at about 10 s after the election plus the 13 to 15 s election time, which is the 20 to 24 s M0-WP12 saw. Part 1 is the defect. A follower the leader has not yet reached is one the quorum commits without, so its failures say nothing about the leader's site. Part 2 alone is the rule working as written: two followers both over 5%. It is a measurement of the apac link, not of this change.

`kill-pre2` (the same code, redeployed for a second sample) had 4 groups with a second change (`-g3`, `-g17`, `-g19`, `-g24`), each with the same shape. B won at 14.2 to 14.7 s and tripped at 21.8 to 25.8 s on A errors of 4 to 8, all `injected`, all before its first ack from A (15.2 to 15.6 s), plus one `deadline` on C in 10 to 13 calls.

Candidate (b) would not have removed `-g22`: its `Noop` committed at 13.7 s, and it tripped at 23.8 s, 10.1 s later, one window and 0.1 s past it. Candidate (a) removes every A failure in all three groups, because every one came before B's first successful call to A.

**Fix: candidate (a)** (`src/raft/member.ts`, `health` and `becomeLeader`). A leader keeps the set of followers that have answered an AppendEntries in its current leadership, cleared when leadership starts. A failed call to a follower outside that set is not counted in `STEP_DOWN`'s window. Once the follower answers, every later call counts as before. This is the smaller change: three lines, and no new timer. It also leaves the gray rule as it was. A leader on a gray site has heard from both followers long before the fault starts, so all its failures still count. Candidate (b) would have been too short for `kill-pre1` `-g22` (above). It would also have hidden a leader that is itself sick for the first 10 s of its term.

## `STEP_DOWN` target on its own link probe, and the hold-10-s variant (2026-10-30)

The design changed the `STEP_DOWN` transfer target twice (`results/m0-wp9.md`, fourth pass item 30 and fifth pass item 34). A target now qualifies on its own link probe: its calls to the other follower fail under `commit_errors`. It does not qualify on the leader's calls to it. It must still be outside RESET_QUARANTINE. Among qualifying targets the leader picks the fewest failed calls, then the most caught up, and it holds if none qualifies. This section implements that rule on `graft-wp4-*`. In the same deploy it measures M0-WP12's open question (`results/m0-wp12.md`, fourth pass, item 14): a new leader ignores the commit-error rule for one 10 s window after winning. That variant applies to groups named `hold10-*` only.

**Change** (`src/raft/member.ts`, `checkHealth`):
- **Before.** The leader picked one target first, by may-lead, quarantine, fewest failed calls and most caught up. It then probed only that target, and held if that one probe failed over 5%.
- **Now.** It probes every follower that may lead and is outside quarantine, in parallel, and keeps those whose probe is at or under `commit_errors`. It transfers to the best of those by fewest failed calls, then most caught up, and holds if none is left.
- **Unreachable target.** A probe call that cannot reach its target does not disqualify that target, as before. On a gray leader's site, the probe call itself fails.
- **Confirm trips.** These now probe too. Before, a slow-storage trip transferred without a probe.
- **The `hold10-` variant** returns early from a commit-error trip while under `STEP_DOWN.windowMs` has passed since `becomeLeader`. The confirm rule is not affected.
- **Run settings.** `run/raft-kill.ts` takes `--prefix` to name the groups. B0-06 used `run/raft-gray.ts` as in F35: 4 groups led on A, 100 writes/s, 30% fault rate, 120 s hold, and 1 s slow confirmations.
- **Local checks** (`.gen/wp4-local.jsonc`, before deploying). B0-06 calls mode with a 40 s hold moved all 4 groups by transfer in 1.9 to 3.0 s, 1 change each, after probing both followers ("b>c fails 0.00, c>b fails 0.00"; `b0-06-calls-local-target1.json`). 240 s of chaos with Porcupine: **Ok** on 29,300 operations, 0 failed, with 5 `STEP_DOWN` transfers in the members' logs (`raft-chaos-local-wp4-target-1.json`).

**B0-06 on the design rule** (deployed to `graft-wp4-*` at 19:17Z; `b0-06-{slow,writes,calls}-tgt-*.json`). "A trip" is A's own leader in term 2: **T** transferred, **H** held, **E** never tripped (moved by election). **The decision cannot be applied as written to a commit-error trip, so I implemented the nearest reading.** A commit-error trip fires only when the leader's calls to *every* follower fail over `commit_errors` (`checkHealth`: `Math.min(...rates) > commitErrors`, as `design/DESIGN.md` `Health` says, "must pass the threshold for every follower"). - **Confirm trip** (slow storage). The leader's calls to the target must be under `commit_errors`. There is no probe. Then the target must be outside quarantine, then fewest failed calls, then most caught up, and the leader holds if none qualifies.
- **Commit-error trip.** The other follower's `linkErrors` probe of the target must read under `commit_errors`. The leader's own reading cannot gate here, because it is over the line for every follower by the trip's definition. A probe that cannot run disqualifies the target. The decision lets an unrunnable probe pass only when the leader's calls pass, and on this trip they never do. Then quarantine, fewest failed calls and most caught up, as above, and the leader holds if none qualifies.
- **`min3-*` groups:** a commit-error trip also needs at least 3 failed calls to each follower in the window.
