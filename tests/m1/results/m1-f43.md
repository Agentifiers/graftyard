# F43: the two replication stalls on the hosted groups

F43 (`decisions/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`) asks what caused two stalls on `repo:196608` on 2026-11-02 (`m1-wp8.md`, fix 4 and "Commit latency during the fault"):

1. Site 0's member stopped at index 235 at 04:49Z with the leader at 237. It stayed there for 25 minutes with every AppendEntries failing, including the 836 bytes of entries 236 to 239.
2. Under the site fault, one replication round on the new leader took 25,024 ms, against a p50 of 191 ms.

**Answer.** Both have one cause. The leader's host dropped every answer that arrived after `CALL_DEADLINE` (1 s). On this install, an AppendEntries carrying a large inline pack takes 1 to 3 s to reach a far follower and come back. A follower that has missed such entries is sent the same call again and again, and every answer is late. The follower applies the call each time, but the leader never learns that, so it keeps `next` where it was and resends the same entries forever. The 836-byte calls were never sent. The leader was still resending entries 172 to 235, which held 1.08 MB, from the first gap on. The fix keeps a late answer for up to 10 s and steps it. A new test fails on the old semantics. On the hosted install, the old code reproduced the slow round twice (16,622 ms and 24,717 ms). The fixed code ran the same scenario with no slow round and no stall.

Everything here ran against the test install `graft-m1-site-a/b/c` and `graft-m1-edge`, and every time is on the member clock. `graft-m1-lin-*` and the lin24 services were not touched.

## What the capture records

`call_peer` in `workers/site/src/host.rs` used to collapse every failure into `Err(false)`, or `Err(true)` for a deadline miss. It now returns `PeerErr { kind, msg }`, and `send` records how every member-to-member call ended.

- **Where.** Each member's `/stats` gets a new `peers` field, keyed by the peer's site id. `/stats?reset=1` clears it with the other counters.
- **What, per peer.**
  - `ok`: calls answered within `CALL_DEADLINE`.
  - `failed`: counts by cause. The causes are `peer` (no member on that site, by the latest config or the bootstrap record), `siteEnv`, `stub`, `request`, `fetch` (the stub's `fetch` threw), `status` (not 200, with the body), `body`, `decode`, `deadline`, and, in `test-faults` builds only, `injected`.
  - `late` and `lateMsMax`: calls that missed the deadline and were answered later. The answer is stepped.
  - `lateFailed`: calls that missed the deadline and then failed, by cause.
  - `errors`: the last 8 failures, each `{at, kind, msg, late, call, entries, bytes, ms}`. `msg` holds the error's text or the status and body, cut to 200 bytes.
  - `appends`: the last 32 AppendEntries calls that carried entries, each `{at, round, first, last, bytes, ms, end}`. `end` is `ok`, `late`, or the failure's cause. A call that missed its deadline shows twice: once as `deadline`, and again when it ended.
- **Cost.** Bounded: two counters maps, 8 errors and 32 appends per peer, and at most two peers per member. Nothing is written to storage.
- **Product build.** The capture is ordinary observability, so it is in the product build. Only `CallFail::Injected` and the injected-fault paths are behind `test-faults`. The product site build has 0 of 42 test needles, and the product edge 0 of 20 (`raw/f43-20261102/checks/grep-*.txt`). The deployed test build has 42 of 42.
- **`observe.ts --stats`** now saves `roundMs` and `peers` too, and prints one line per member and peer: the counts, then each kept error, then the last append. `observe.ts` samples also carry each member's `last`.

`failedCalls` keeps its old meaning, counting calls that left and failed, deadline misses included. Injected failures are not counted.

## Root cause

### Stall 1: site 0's member of `repo:196608` held at 235

Workers Logs for `graft-m1-site-a` still held 04:44 to 05:48Z. The stuck member is Durable Object `47eb5da6…` (`repo:196608#1#f0`). The raw events and tables are in `raw/f43-20261102/` (`ev-47eb-*`, `t47raft.tsv`, `t47b.tsv`).

- The member was not refusing anything. With the fault off (04:49:58 to 04:53:20 and 05:16:05 to 05:47Z) it answered 424 `/raft` calls, all with **200**. 414 of those took at least 1 s on the callee, with a p50 of 1,999 ms and a max of 2,929 ms. The 1,426 answers of 503 all fall inside fault windows (`member class failing (injected site fault)`).
- The calls came back to back: each began about when the previous one ended (the gap between arrivals had a mode of 2,001 ms). Most carried a large body: the 339 calls that used at least 15 ms of CPU had wall times from 613 to 2,929 ms, p50 1,999 ms.
- What the leader was resending. The site-0 member had been cut off since t-251's fault started at 04:05Z, at index 171. Entries 172 to 235 were 1,080,731 bytes in all (`log-196608-160-239.json`). Entries 171, 173, 175 and 176 hold inline packs of 523 kB, 262 kB, 524 kB and 262 kB. So when the fault cleared, the catch-up call from `prev` 171 carried `CATCHUP_MAX` entries, 172 to 235, about 1.08 MB, within `APPEND_BYTES_MAX` (1.5 MB). That is why the member stopped at exactly 235.
- Why it never finished. The follower stored the entries and answered `ok, matched: 235`, about 2 s after the send. The leader's host had already given up at 1 s (`within(CALL_DEADLINE, call).unwrap_or(Err(true))`), so the answer was dropped. The leader called `call_failed`, kept `next` at 172, and `tick` sent the same 1.08 MB call again. The follower matched every entry by digest and answered `ok` again, about 2 s later, and the answer was dropped again. The leader never learned `matched: 235`, so it never sent 236 to 239. The 836-byte calls in fix 4 were never made.
- Why it held at 235. The follower's commit index comes from the leader's AppendEntries, capped at what the call carried (`set_commit(req.commit.min(matched))`). Every call carried entries through 235, so `/status` showed 235 and never moved.
- The 10 fast answers (102 to 639 ms, about 1 ms of CPU) were small calls, not the catch-up call. The logs do not say which message they carried. The six `home.sh` transfers in that window each needed the target caught up, so each was abandoned with `Busy`.
- The redeploy at 05:47Z reset every member. The new leader started its rounds from its own last index. The follower refused them with `last: 235`, and the leader backed `next` up to 236. So the next catch-up call carried only 236 onward, a few hundred bytes, which answered in time. The follower caught up in 16 s.

`Member::call_failed` in `crates/raft` already allows this: "A reply that still arrives later may be passed to `step`; the call counts once, as failed." The core accepts a late `AppendRes` and advances `matched` from it (checked with a scratch scenario, deleted after). The fault was in the host, not in `crates/raft`, so `crates/raft` is unchanged.

### Stall 2: the 25,024 ms round

This is the same mechanism with the leader on a site that holds a quorum. In t-251, `repo:196608`'s leader moved to site 1 at 04:05:42Z. Its third entry, 175, holds a 524 kB inline pack. The apac member (`743a6a46`, site 2) logs show it:

- 04:10:11 to 04:10:35: 14 `/raft` calls, one every 2 s, each with 1,762 to 1,785 ms of wall time. That is entry 175 being applied and answered late, again and again.
- 04:10:44: one call answered in 143 ms, and the observer shows `c175` on the leader at 04:10:35 (round end) and on site 2 at 04:10:43.

The round finished only when an attempt happened to answer within 1 s. With site 0 failed, the quorum needs site 2's ack, so each lost answer held the commit. This took 25 s that time. Nothing bounds it.

### The design premise this contradicts

DESIGN, Shape, the `STEP_DOWN` doc: "a far follower misses CALL_DEADLINE about once in 10 to 15 calls with no fault". That holds for small calls. An AppendEntries with an inline pack of about 0.5 to 1 MB missed `CALL_DEADLINE` to a far follower on most attempts in these runs. In the fixed soak below, the kept AppendEntries rings hold 365 calls of 500 kB or more that ended at the deadline or in time, and 230 of them (63%) missed it. The design's sentence assumes a missed call is then lost. The rule was written for that case, and the host dropped late answers to match it.

## The fix

`workers/site/src/host.rs`:

- `answer(call, deadline, limit, missed)` races the call against `CALL_DEADLINE`. A miss runs `missed` at once: the failure is recorded, and `Member::call_failed(deadline = true)` runs, as before, so `STEP_DOWN`'s window and the core's resend timing are unchanged. The call is then awaited for up to `LATE_LIMIT` more. That limit is 10 s [guess]. The slowest late answer seen was 8,802 ms, in the soak below, so 10 s has little margin. An answer that arrives in that time is returned as `Late` and stepped.
- `send` builds the call body once, drops the envelope before the await, and steps a late answer's envelopes as it steps an in-time answer's. A late failure is recorded and does not call `call_failed` a second time.
- `call_peer` returns `PeerErr` with the cause and its text, and reads a non-200 answer's body into it.

The test is `host::tests::an_answer_after_the_deadline_is_kept_and_the_miss_reported_first` in `workers/site`. It checks that the miss is reported at the deadline, that the call is still awaited, and that its answer comes back as `Late`. A second test checks the in-time and past-the-limit cases. With `answer` set back to the old semantics (return `Missed` at the deadline), the first test fails with `left: Ready(Missed) right: Pending` ("still waiting for the answer"). The fixed code passes.

`tests/m1/wp8/f43-lag.sh` holds a follower out and then lets it back in. It puts the leader of `wp6/f43a` (`repo:196624`, new) on one site and fails site 0's member class with `fault.sh`. It then pushes 6 to 8 commits, each adding 700 to 900 kB of random bytes, so each push is one entry with an inline pack. Then it clears the fault and watches for 180 s. `observe.ts` samples every 2 s.

In `lag-new-apac`, the leader's capture shows each round's answers from site 1 (weur), 1.23 MB per call, missing the deadline and coming back late. Of 51 calls, 24 missed and all 24 were answered late, the slowest in 3,326 ms. Every one was stepped. The round finished on the first answer instead of the first in-time answer.
- Stall 1 did not recur on the old code here. The held-out follower caught up within 6 to 10 s, because each catch-up call carried at most two of these entries. The old code catches up as soon as one attempt answers within 1 s. In the original, entries 172 to 235 made a 1.08 MB call that needed about 2 s, and no attempt in 25 minutes was fast enough. ## The soak

`tests/m1/wp8/f43-soak.sh`, as the orb service `f43-soak`, on the fixed build. It ran on four repositories: `wp6/f43a`, `wp6/f43b`, `wp6/f43c` (196624 to 196626, seeded for this) and `wp6/main` (196608). Each cycle takes about 3 to 4 minutes:

- Every repository's leader moves to site `cycle mod 3` (`home.sh`).
- One chaos step, by `cycle mod 4`. 0: none. 1: total fault on a follower site. 2: gray fault (`FAULT_RATE=0.3`) on the leader's site. 3: `/reset` of a follower's member on every repository.
- During the step, each repository takes 2 pushes of a 700 kB blob and 4 small pushes, all four repositories in parallel.
- Then the fault clears. After 60 s, every follower's stored `last` is checked against its leader's commit index. Every member's `/stats` is read with `reset=1`, so each `stats-<cycle>.jsonl` holds one cycle's calls.

`tests/m1/wp8/f43-summary.py` reduces it. The soak ran from 19:28:45 to 20:21:54Z (53 minutes, 14 cycles: 4 follower faults, 4 gray faults on the leader's site, 3 follower resets, 3 with no chaos). ### A different defect the soak found: restore runs out of memory on a log of large entries

`repo:196624` (`wp6/f43a`) took 53 pushes of 0.7 to 1 MB inline packs across the reproduction runs and the soak. Its site-c member first crashed while constructing at 19:42:12Z and again at 19:54:27Z. Each time it reinitialized and recovered. From 20:06Z all three members crashed repeatedly. Workers Logs (`raw/f43-20261102/soak-logs-site-*.json`) show 39 `Critical error`s, all at `Uint8Array::copy_to` under `host::rows` in `Host::restore` (`load`). Site a logged `Durable Object's isolate exceeded its memory limit and was reset.` at 20:19:48Z. Site c logged 45 `Durable Object is overloaded` while requests queued behind the failing constructor. At 20:22Z all three members of `repo:196624` answered no `/status`. The other three soak repositories were healthy.

`load` reads every log row above `applied − LOG_WINDOW` (1,000 entries) into memory and decodes each one. The fold's `request` rows hold the same events, inline packs included. A group with about 50 MB of inline packs in its log therefore needs several copies of them in a 128 MB isolate at restore [inference from the stack, the message, and the log's size]. This is not F43 and not caused by this change, which does not touch `load`, `restore` or the window. The defect is that `LOG_WINDOW` bounds the in-memory log by entry count only. DESIGN's entry limit is 1.5 MB, so 1,000 entries can be 1.5 GB. Fixing it needs a byte bound on both the host's `load` and `Member::set_log_window` in `crates/raft`, so it is left as a finding. `repo:196624` stays wedged on the test install until a member can restore.

## Design sentences the evidence contradicts (quoted, not edited)

1. DESIGN, Shape, `STEP_DOWN`'s doc: "a far follower misses CALL_DEADLINE about once in 10 to 15 calls with no fault". This is true for small calls. AppendEntries carrying 0.5 to 1.9 MB of inline packs missed it on 63% of the calls kept in the soak's rings, and on most attempts in the original stall. With large entries the commit-error rule sees far more misses than this sentence budgets for. In `lag-new-apac` it moved the leader once after the fault cleared. Whether `CALL_DEADLINE` should scale with the call's size, or deadline misses on large AppendEntries should count differently, is for the design.
2. DESIGN, "Commit and read paths": "Every receipt and member-to-member call has a 1 s deadline. The host sends each member-to-member message once and never retries it". With the fix, the 1 s deadline still decides when the core hears of a miss, but the host now keeps waiting up to `LATE_LIMIT` (10 s) for the answer. The text does not say what happens to an answer after the deadline. The old code dropped it, and that is what caused both stalls.
3. DESIGN, Shape: `ENTRY_MAX` / "Entry under 1.5 MB against the 2 MB row limit" together with `LOG_WINDOW = 1_000` ("Applied entries a Durable Object member keeps in memory", `crates/raft/src/lib.rs`). A count of 1,000 entries of up to 1.5 MB is not a memory bound for a 128 MB isolate. A log of about 50 entries with 1 MB inline packs already made restore exceed the isolate's memory on this install.
4. `m1-wp8.md` fix 4: "The stuck follower's missing entries 236 to 239 were 836 bytes in all, so the byte cap does not explain that member." The byte cap is not the explanation, but the calls that failed were not 836 bytes. They were the 1.08 MB resend of 172 to 235, which fits under the cap and still takes longer than `CALL_DEADLINE`.
