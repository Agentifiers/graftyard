## 1. Shed-backoff's effect

| | m51 | m52 |
|---|---|---|
| edge 5xx (load window) | 18,294 | 14,191 (13,058 to 14,191 across two reads of the same window: the events view is sampled late) |
| wall time of the 5xx | p50 22.0 s (REST and GraphQL writes, every one ≥ 19 s) | **p50 4.9 s, p99 9.0 s**; task 5.2 s, renew 5.3 s, GraphQL 5.2 s, label 5.3 s, lease 5.2 s, upload-pack 3.6 s |
| learner `/read` per learner, after the leader change | 459, 489, 503, 155 /s | **115, 211, 144, 202 /s** |
| learner `/read` per learner, before the change | 59, 36, 26, 70 /s | 82, 131, 111, 120 /s |

[measured, `tests/fanout/fivexxwall.py` on Workers Logs; `tests/fanout/learnerrates.py` on `raw/tsave-m52b33.jsonl`]

Every 5xx is a 503 from the edge (`fivexxwall.py`): task 4,826, renew 2,582, GraphQL 2,483, upload-pack 1,724, label
1,722, lease 840, receive-pack 13, merge 1. There were no 500s, so the lease-render fix holds (m51: 228 upload-pack 500
and 44 renew 500).

So shed-backoff did what it was built for. A write gives up at about 4 s plus its last call, not 20 s: the p50 fell
from 22.0 s to 4.9 s. Learner reads after the leader change fell from 136 to 514 /s to 115 to 211 /s, so the retry
storm is gone. Before the change they ran higher than m51's (82 to 131 /s against 26 to 70 /s), because m52's leader
was loaded from the start (below) and writes retried sheds for their 4 s. The 503 count fell only 22%: the leader was
stalled for most of the run, so those writes still had no read to get.

**Reading, keeping the causes apart.**

- **Refs pages (F91) are new load, but not the stall's lead stream.** They are 36.9 /s on m52's leader and 0 on m51's.
  That is one per ls-refs (offered 33 /s; `lsRefsBy`: 8,125 answered at the edge, 1,768 × 503 from the shard path).
  They lead the stall weakly (0.22) and less than the commit path or learner `/raft` (0.41 to 0.43). They are 9% of the
  leader's inbound. [inference] Their open count at entry (p50 514) is the same as every other stream's, so they wait in
  the same queue rather than set it.
- **Learner `/raft` is about the same as m51's** (72.4 against 65.5 /s, 4 learners each). It now leads the stall as
  much as the commit path does (0.41 against 0.19 on m51), because the leader is stalled more often.
- **The commit path doubled**, and `/outcome_by_request` asks are the new part: 58.7 /s in m52's first 50 s against
  0 in m51's. An ask is the receipt's call when a `/commit` send has no reply within its `RECEIPT_OPEN`. So m52's
  leader answered sends late from the start, and each late send added an ask. [inference] It is a symptom of the same
  queue, as the asks were on m50 and m51, not a separate stream to cut.
- **What made m52's leader slower from the first ten seconds** is not settled by this run. The offered load is
  identical (`offered`: 256.3 group commands/s, 33 ref reads/s). m52 adds refs pages (+37 /s) and a different learner
  placement. The leader's syncs were slow from +10 s: 3 to 6 syncs of 0.5 s or more per 10 s from the start, max
  2.6 to 3.4 s, against none in m51's first 50 s. One run on each build cannot separate F91's refs load from storage
  luck. B0-02's F91 A/B (run 7) is the test that can.

## 3. Leader changes and their reasons

One change, by the confirm rule. Site a's member logged 13 `StepDown { why: Confirm .. , to: None }` alarms from
14:44:41 to 14:47:30Z (p99 913 to 1,690 ms, 6 to 10 slow of 7 to 24). Each held, because no follower qualified as a
target. The 14th, at 14:48:08.783Z (`p99_ms: 813, slow: 6, of: 24, to: Some(SiteId(1))`), transferred to site b.

- **The slow-sync explanation matches.** Site a's `Confirm` rows (one per `sync()`) while it led: 320 syncs, 36 of them
  1 s or longer (9.5 a minute), max 4,144 ms. In the 10 s before the transfer: 784, 764, 1,000 and 1,094 ms among 22.
  Sites b and c, followers then, had no sync over 138 ms. After the transfer, site b as leader had 12 syncs of 1 s or
  more in 71 (10.0 a minute, max 3,806 ms), and site a, now a follower, had none over 146 ms. So the slow syncs follow
  the leadership, as on m50 and m51 (`tests/am1/results/health-gate-lease.md`, "Why those syncs were slow"), and the
  handoff traded one loaded store for another: the case F92 records.
- **The new leader then alarmed 9 times in 81 s** (14:48:19 to 14:49:30Z): 5 `Confirm` and 4 `CommitErrors` (calls to
  both followers failing 7 to 14 times in the window), all `to: None`, so it held.
- **No `suspect`-driven election.** The edge sent 69 `suspect` reports in all (m51: 906). The read gate's backoff and
  the receipt's busy hold kept reports down.

## Which lever next

The leader is not loaded by learner `/raft` more than on m51. Learner `/raft` is 72 /s against m51's 66, and 18% of
the leader's inbound against 28%. So the orchestrator's first branch ("If the leader is still loaded by learner /raft,
test LEARNER_READ_EVERY longer") does not apply on this evidence. I did not run it, and I did not name a latency
budget for it.

What the run does show:

1. **The commit path is the leader's load**: 68.5% of its inbound, with asks a symptom of late sends.
2. **Refs pages are 9% and new.** B0-02's F91 A/B decides whether moving them off the leader is the step that brings
   B0-33 back to m51's start. Until that A/B, refs pages and the commit path cannot be told apart as causes of m52's
   slower first minute.
3. **The confirm rule moves the leadership onto storage just as slow**, as F92 records. That cost one change here, and
   B0-33's zero-change line fails on it alone.
