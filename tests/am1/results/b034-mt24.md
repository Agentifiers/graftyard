## The home's rehydrate

[measured: Workers Logs `GitShard` on `graft-m1-site-a`, "shard rehydrate <net>" (each step's cumulative `ms`, `stored`'s
spools, `index_ms`, `midx_ms`); `shardprocs-*.jsonl` for site 0 (`maint.running`, process list), on Cloudflare's clock]

**Short answer.** About half of the rehydrate is maintenance it waits for, not rehydrate work. Once it has applied the
refs, the rehydrate's catch-up starts the container's geometric repack. The backstop check that follows then waits for

whose cause is not read.

**1. The backstop waits out the repack.** This is about 164 s of mt24's 330 [measured times; the mechanism is read
from the code].
- In every home run, the container's repack starts within one 15 s sample after `refs` ends. It runs at RSS up to
  7.9 GB, followed by a `commit-graph write`, and the rehydrate logs done within 12 s of the last sample that shows it
  running. The warm run, with no repack, spent 15.7 s on 75 entries.
- The repack's start: after the ref pages, the rehydrate catches up from the first page's index (`Mode::CatchUp`). Each
  page applies its ref changes with `POST /refs` (`HostApi::apply_refs`), which sends no `repack` flag. The container
  treats a POST as `done` and calls `startRepack` unless `repack=0` (`containers/git/server/main.go`, `handleRefs`).
  The home's new container is at client CPU 0.41 and unheld, so `maintBegin` grants it.
- The wait: the rehydrate skipped the 246 packs that promotions cover, so the backstop runs `/connected?repack=1`. Its
  walk takes `repacking.Lock()` (`connectedWalk`), so it waits for the whole repack and commit-graph. Only after that
  does `rehydrate` drop its place and log done.
- The ref pages' own `PUT` already sends `repack=0` for this case, so that the repack starts after the walk
  (`lm1-memory.md`, round 21). The catch-up's `POST`s undo that.

**2. mt24 against mt21: 122 s more.**
- 65 s is the longer repack. It started at 189 packs against 127; `pack-objects` ran about 105 s against 70, and
  commit-graph about 35 s against 15.
- 30 s is the same 58 stored packs spooling at p50 3.92 s against 2.24 [measured].
- 28 s is the tail: 141 entries against 84, and `index_ms` 40.5 s against 16.6.
- Why R2's reads and `index-pack` ran slower is not read [unverified].

**3. Without the wait** [inference]: mt24's rehydrate would end at about 166 s (`refs`), plus a 20 to 25 s catch-up at
the warm run's 0.15 to 0.21 s an entry, plus the walk. That is about 190 s instead of 330, and mt21's about 125 s
instead of 208. The fold base after it took about 45 s (the `/data/compose/` `pack-objects` at 17:25:33 to 17:26:03).
So mt24's first post-kill part would have landed near +235 s, before b switched to `composed` at +289 s.

true`, disk `4ba33e`). A second record, done at 13:09:29Z (448.1 s, fresh, disk `99feba`), started at the kill. Its
`refs` end (13:05:01.5) and done line bracket the sampled container's repack (13:05:17 to 13:09:17) to within one

## G37: the design choice

The freeze on mt24 is the home's rehydrate (330 s, half of it the repack wait above) plus the due fold's base (about
45 s). The chain's buckets, a and b, held a whole chain throughout: a base and 20 parts, tip at 17:18. b had installed
that chain after its own catch-ups (`chain-install`), and composed from it past a tip that stopped moving.

**C. Builder failover (rejected in `g37-design.md`).** While the home's first shard cannot step, the first caught-up
shard on the other chain site becomes the builder and writes the buckets' chain. The home takes the role back once it
can step.
- **The second-writer risk.** The edge's chain reads are safe only because one writer orders the puts:
  - every file is put before the head that names it;
  - an old base's files are deleted only after the head that replaces it (`colo-gate`, `head-etags`, `chain-warm`).

  Two builders that fold at different times can each delete files the other's head names. An edge then streams a head
  whose pack is gone and cuts the clone after bytes have left (the case `chain-hold` and `chain-warm` exist to stop).
  The handover is the same race: b steps until the home's first step lands. Making it safe needs a lease or a fence on
  `head`, which is a new failure domain with rows in "Failure modes" and "Deployment modes".
- **On mt24.** b would step after its own catch-ups, at nice 19 on a container at client CPU 0.99 to 1.00. It would
  also fold at the 20th step, a 260 MB base build of about 17 to 20 CPU-s, on the busiest site.

**E. The other chain site steps a local chain from the buckets' chain while the home rehydrates, and writes nothing.**
b's shard already installs the buckets' chain. While that chain's tip is behind HEAD and the home cannot step, b's
container steps parts from the installed tip to HEAD on its own disk, with no puts to R2. It composes misses from that
chain: the chain's packs plus a small pack of the want's own new objects, not one built against a frozen tip. When the
home's next head lands in the buckets, b installs it as now. `/compose-head` drops every file the head does not name,
and a miss that has a dropped file open keeps reading it.
- **The second-writer risk.** None in R2, by construction: only the builder path in `ready_chain` puts, and E adds no
  put. The edge's `tail` manifests still name only bucket packs. So a want past the frozen bucket tip is answered by b's
  container whole (`composed`), as now, but with a small want pack.
- **What it adds.**
  - A part step on b for each tip move during the freeze, at nice 19 on the moved-load site. A part's wall there is not
    measured [unverified].
  - A rule that b never folds locally, so the 260 MB base build stays on the home. A local chain then grows by one part
    per tip move until the home's head lands.
  - One test against a planted fold or put on b.
- **What it does not fix.** The home's own misses: it answers `warming` until its rehydrate ends, as now.

**Recommendation** [inference for every price]:
1. **First, take the repack wait out of the rehydrate**, at $0 and with no design change. The rehydrate's catch-up
   would send `repack=0` on its ref writes when the backstop follows, as the ref put already does, so the backstop's
   walk runs first and starts the repack itself. By the estimate in "Without the wait", that cuts the freeze from about
   375 s to about 235 s on mt24, under b's switch at +289 s. It is a shard-image and site-script change on one site day.
   It touches neither G41's hold nor the chain's writers.
2. **Then E, if a later row still shows composes past a frozen chain.** It keeps one writer. mt23's 448 s freeze would
   still be about 180 s even without the wait.
3. **Not C.** It gains what E gains and adds a second writer with a fence.
