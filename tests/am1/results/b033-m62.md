## 1. Why readsMax and answerBytes rose on 192 and 195

**Not a shared isolate.** The tsave `/isolate` probes give 8 distinct isolate ids for the 3 voters and 4 learners, and
`rows.sh` prints "shared: none". No learner shares an isolate with a voter or with another learner. So B0-02's case,
two members of one repository in one isolate sharing `crate::feed::FEEDS` (main afd0f46b), did not happen on m62.

**Isolate-wide state on the learner read path.** There are three such maps:
- `DUMPS` (`workers/site/src/family.rs`) is keyed by `RepoId`, and so is `NARROW`.
- `REBUILT` and `NARROW_BUILT` are keyed by `RepoId`, and by `(RepoId, query)` for `NARROW_BUILT`.
- READS_MAX accounting is not isolate-wide: `reads_in_flight`, `heavy_reads` and `answers` live on the `Host`, one per
  member.

Sharing `DUMPS` or `NARROW` across members of one repository is safe by design. A dump records the fold it was built
from (`DumpAt`: applied, loaded, child), and `covers` serves it only to a read whose floor it covers on the same kind
of fold. `REBUILT` would let one member's rebuild delay another's, but only in a shared isolate, which m62 did not have.

**What changed on 192 and 195: ticker stalls.** These are `Trace::Lag` rows of 500 ms or more, inside the load window:

| learner | site | stall s | stalls of 2 s or more | `/read`s entered | spread share | CPU s/min (Cloudflare) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 192 | a | **68.4** | 15 (55.2 s) | 34,092 | 27% | 29–41 |
| 195 | a | **78.2** | 16 (63.1 s) | 31,447 | 25% | 33–42 |
| 193 | b | 2.0 | 0 | 26,640 | 22% | 22–39 |
| 194 | c | 0 | 0 | 24,086 | 20% | 12–30 |

m61 had 192 at 51.4 s, 193 at 3.0 s, and 195 and 194 at 0.

- **readsMax follows the stalls.** All of 192's readsMax and answerBytes sheds came in its last minute, and 195's in
  minutes 2 and 4. Those are the minutes with its stalls: 192 had 30.7 s and 25.9 s of lag in minutes 3 and 4, and
  195 had 9.7, 23.1 and 41.6 s in minutes 2 to 4. A read that arrives during a stall waits for the stall to end.
  Requests open at the stamp before a 1 s gap were p50 132 on 192 and 108 on 195, against 72 and 89 over the row.
  `Entered` showed up to 247 open, against READS_MAX 256.
- **answerBytes follows the stalls too** [inference]. `Answers` counts the distinct bytes of answers still streaming,
  and a stalled isolate does not pull its streams. Learners still rebuild every LEARNER_DUMP_EVERY_MS, 500 ms, once
  the floor passes the cached dump. Each stream held past a rebuild keeps an older copy alive. At m62's late dump of
  2.4 to 2.6 MB, about 7 such copies reach ANSWER_BYTES_MAX (16 MB). On m61 the rebuild gate shed these reads before
  they streamed, so answerBytes was 1,867. On m62, dump-floor serves them, so more streams stay open.
- **The stalls are not the learners' own CPU** [inference, from Cloudflare's per-object CPU]. 192 and 195 used 29 to
  42 CPU seconds a minute. 193 used 35 to 39 in the same minutes with no stalls. A straight-line fit of CPU against
  dump MB streamed, MB built and narrow reads explains none of it (R² 0.01 to 0.04, 17 to 44 learner-minutes). What
  the stalls do share:
  - They are on site a's two learners: 25.1 s of 192's stall time overlaps 195's. The leader's 10 s of stall, also on
    site a, overlaps neither.
  - They open on a replication step. 53% to 58% of the ≥1 s gaps start at a stamp with an `Applied`, `Stepped` and
    `AppendStepped` row, against 5% to 6% of all stamps.
  - They overlap slow storage confirms. 17 of 192's 24 stalls and 25 of 195's 28 overlap a confirm of 500 ms or more.
    Their confirm p99 was 1,791 and 1,967 ms, against 655 on b and 122 on c.
  - A late timer with no CPU charged to the object is what a descheduled isolate or a contended host would show. The
    traces cannot tell those apart.
- **Site a got more of the reads.** Learners on a took 51% of spread reads (median edge isolate 56%), against 47% on
  m61. `timed_spread_order` prefers learners within ROUTE_MARGIN (200 ms) of the best answer time. For the
  load's edge isolates, a's learners are the nearest, and 192 was the top learner for 128 of 200 isolates. A shed
  answer scores FOLLOWER_CALL (2 s) and moves the learner down, but only until ROUTE_STALE (10 s) and the ROUTE_SPREAD
  pad bring it back. So the busiest learners are the ones that stall, and the 2 s retry sends the shed read back to
  the same short list [inference].
- **The offered bytes are large.** Dump reads streamed 1.5 to 16.5 GB per learner per minute on m62 (packed reads ×
  dump size). In 10 s buckets above 150 MB/s offered, a learner was lagged 3.4 to 5.3 s of the 10 on m62, against 1.0
  to 4.1 s on m61. Below 100 MB/s, lag was under 0.2 s. On 192 and 195, 67.6 of 70.5 and 74.9 of 82.9 stall seconds
  fall in such buckets. 193 and 194 had 5 and 2 such buckets with almost no lag. So a high byte rate is needed for a
  stall but does not cause one by itself. Site a adds the rest.

**So:** readsMax and answerBytes are not DUMPS holding bigger answers per repository: one dump per repository, at
most 2.57 MB, was cached on any voter, and the learners' own `memSplit` did not answer. They are not the shared-isolate
case either. They are reads and streams piling up on the two site-a learners during ticker stalls of seconds. Those
two learners carry the most dump bytes, because routing favours them. The stalls are not their own CPU: they line up
with replication steps and slow storage confirms on site a [measured: the stalls, the overlaps and the CPU; the
host-contention reading is inference].

## 2. Why member /read per request rose from 2.22 to 2.53

`/tmp/rpr.py` divides member `/read`s by the plan's offered tier-read rate, 168.8 a second, which is the same on both
rows. On m62, a1 actually made more requests:
- m61: 171.1 a second;
- m62: 187.5 a second.

m62 made 34,564 side requests against 31,361, and 8,740 `read:ref` against 6,928. a1's scheduler caps each kind at 400
in flight (`every`, `tests/am1/rest/a1.mjs`). On m61, `read:ref` p99 was 78,952 ms, so it hit the cap 877 times. On
m62 that p99 was 12,845 ms, with no cap skips, and side requests ended sooner.

| | m61 | m62 |
|---|---|---|
| member `/read` in the window | 112,536 | 128,249 |
| per offered request (168.8/s) | 2.22 | 2.53 |
| per request a1 made | 2.19 | **2.28** |
| learner sheds (memguard) | 60,771 | 62,383 |

So **about 70% of the rise is the denominator: more requests made, not more reads per request.** The rest, 2.19 →
2.28, is retries. By minute, `/read` per request made rose to 3.31 and 3.52 in m62's last two minutes, as the share
of side requests ending 503 rose to 43% and 55%. Each shed write takes one backed-off retry pass (SHED_RETRY_FOR 2 s).
The query mix barely moved: per offered request, packed 0.8 → 1.91 and core 0.32 → 0.83 by minute on m62, against
0.64 → 1.63 and 0.34 → 0.70 on m61.

## Ranked change

**1. The edge's lease, renew and task commands read the core tier narrowly, not the packed whole-tier dump.**
- **Why these.** `task`, `renew` and `lease` were 9,604 of the 15,040 tier-shed 503s (5,755, 2,881 and 968). Their
  requests (taskEdit, standIn, leaseRenew, lease) were 22,347 of 34,564 side requests. They decide on the core tier
  alone:
  - `agents.rs` leases call `read_core`, which is `Rows::Core`;
  - `tasks` reads core and metadata, but its PATCH and cancel decide on `agent_task` and the lease tables.
- **Why the dump is the cost.** `Rows::Core` still calls `tier_rows(c, &repo, None)`: the packed dump of both tiers, at
  2.4 to 2.6 MB by the row's end. The edge then drops the metadata tier (`served_meta(.., core_only, ..)`). So about
  two thirds of the learners' dump reads stream a metadata tier that the request discards.
- **The change.** A core-only rows read, such as `{"rows": {"core": true}}`, built and cached per floor like the dump,
  for `Rows::Core`. Task PATCH and cancel would go on to it too, if their reply can render from core plus the one
  task's `agent_task_meta` row. That reply needs checking first.
- **Design first.** It belongs with "Follower read" in `design/DESIGN.md`, as `access` and `agent` rows already do.
  It also needs a DECISIONS entry with a flip: revert if any core read misses a row that a command's `decide` needs.
- **Linearizability** is the same as for the access and agent rows: read-index at a floor.
- **Expected effect** [model guess]. The metadata tier is what grows with comments and labels, so the core dump should
  be a small fraction of the 2.5 MB [inference; not measured].
  - Learners' streamed bytes would fall by about two thirds.
  - Every learner would stay below the 100 MB/s offered at which m61 and m62 showed under 0.2 s of lag in 10 s.
  - The site-a stalls' readsMax and answerBytes sheds would go with it (35k of m62's 62k learner sheds).
  - Edge tier 503s: from 15,040 to about 4,000 to 7,000.
  - Writes ending 503: from 31% to about 12% to 18%.
  - Agent ok/s: from 61.9 to about 70 to 80. Agent pushes don't use this read, but the leader's held-commit burst
    and admission still bound them.

**2. Lower: spread reads away from a stalling learner for longer.** For example, a shed or a late answer keeps its
learner out of the near set for ROUTE_STALE rather than one sample. It would spread site a's 51% more evenly. It does
not cut bytes, so it ranks below 1.

**Overlap with B0-02's FEEDS fix: none on m62's evidence.** No learner shared an isolate. FEEDS is the activity feed's
waiters, gap, ring and applied_at, not the tier read path. The read path's isolate-wide maps (DUMPS, NARROW, REBUILT,
NARROW_BUILT) are keyed by repository by design, and are safe to share between members of one repository through
`DumpAt::covers` and the narrow floor check. Keying FEEDS per member changes none of them. One caveat: if two members
of a repository ever share an isolate, `REBUILT` would let one member's rebuild space out the other's. That costs
latency, never correctness. It is worth one line in B0-02's review, not a change here.
