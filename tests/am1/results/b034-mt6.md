## Deeper analysis

### 1. Where the clone TTFB tail goes

**The method.** Each miss logs one `shard miss` line (`raw/misslines-mt6.jsonl`) with four times:
- `ready_ms`: the wait for this shard's chain step;
- `caught_up_ms`: the wait for a catch-up;
- `headers_ms`: the container's headers;
- the answer.

The shard's clock reads about 12.1 s ahead of the orb's (`misses.py`'s `to_shard` median), so probes are joined to
lines by arrival, after removing that offset. Times inside one line come from one clock.

| Phase | Misses | Answers | Chain wait p99 / max | Catch-up p99 | Container to headers p99 / max | 503 at the deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| j9 | 49 | tail 49 | 0 | | 1.9 / 1.9 s | 0 |
| kill | 26 | tail 25, composed 1 | 14.2 / 14.2 s | 1.9 s | 0.7 / 0.7 s | 0 |

keeps no log].

### 3. The second network's human push: 20.4 s on the client against 10.3 s on the edge

**These are two different pushes.**

**The 20.4 s client sample.** It starts at 18:22:09.6. Its POST (`mwnv6rdr-2676`, 200 ok) began 18.56 s later and
took 1.80 s, with edge `total` 1,777 ms. The 18.56 s before the POST is a1's `tip(ref)`, a v2 `ls-refs` sent before
every human push:
- **At the edge:** that `ls-refs` ran 18,525 ms of wall time on 30 ms of CPU.
- **At the shard:** `1502d838`, in its post-rehydrate window, answered a plain `/upload-pack` (no `fill`) in 11,310 ms
  that ended 0.26 s before the edge's.

So the edge's leader-free read took about 7 s and failed over to the shard. The shard then waited for its catch-up
[inference: Workers Logs do not link the two invocations]. Over the row, a1 sent 30,324 `ls-refs` the edge answered,
and 95 the shard answered with 200, 26 with 503 and 70 with no status.

**The tip read in every phase.** It takes p50 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.4 / 1.5 s and p99 2.8 / 2.9 / 18.6 / 4.3 s before the
POST. A real `git push` makes no upload-pack `ls-refs`: it reads the receive-pack advertisement, which the edge answers
itself. So the client clock's human push includes one read a real push does not make. That bears on A33's question;
the gate is unchanged.

**The 10.3 s edge sample.** It is a different push, at 18:21:52, with validate 9,209 ms:
- `1502d838` answered its validates 503 `warming` at once while it rehydrated.
- The pool shard `39f1a2e4` answered 409.
- The validates that came after got 200 from `1502d838` in 9.2 to 15.1 s, once its rehydrate ended.

That push's client sample is 11.8 s.

### The ranked fix

**First: bound `Compose::ready` to about 2 s when another site follows (`next=1`), then answer 503 `warming`.**
- **The change:** `workers/site/src/shard.rs`, `/upload-pack`: `crate::host::within(left(), compose.ready())`
  becomes a short wait when `next=1`, and the last site keeps the whole deadline.
- **Why:** today a non-final shard whose chain has not stepped holds the miss for the edge's full 15 s home wait,
  while the next site already holds a synced chain and composes in under 1 s. The existing `warming_now` already does
  this for a rehydrating shard, so the change carries that rule into the chain wait.
- **Expected effect on clone TTFB p99:**
  - second 22.6 to about 14.3 s and hostile 19.6 to about 12.8 s, from the 32 matched probes losing 15 s (plus about
    2 s each);
  - j9 unchanged at 4.3 s;
  - kill unknown. Kill's 14 s chain waits were on site b, the second try, so they would move on to site c, whose
    bucket held no chain files in this row. Kill could get better or worse.
- **What it does not reach:** it does not bring any phase near 1.1 s on the client clock. Hits alone are p99 5.2 s
  through the orb (A33).

**The alternative:** let the builder install the chain head already in R2 after a fresh container, instead of
stepping a new base at nice 19. That takes out the 13 s `ready_ms` on `1502d838` at its source, but it is a larger
change in `ready_chain`.
