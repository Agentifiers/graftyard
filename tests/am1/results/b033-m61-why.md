# B0-33 m61r33: why 503s rose with the leader healthy

Code reading and the logs and traces of m61r33 (repo 131375, load 19:40:21.6Z to 19:45:21Z, a1 run `mwnybobj`),
against m60r33 (131374, `mwnnkow7`) and m56b33 (131370, `mwn8854t`). No hosted run. Tags follow
`design/SHARED.md`, "Read this first".

m61 sent 16,112 × 503, and 15,375 of 44,198 writes ended 503 (34.8%). For m60 the figures were 9,901 and 19.7%. By
class (`raw/fivexxwhy-m61.txt`):
- tier rows "every member asked shed": 15,170 (94%);
- access rows shed: 456;
- shard `warming`: 486.

So the tier sheds carry the rise, and question 2 decides the ranking.

## 1. Shard warming, 14 → 486

- **Which shards, and when.** The edge's 503s by minute were warming 281 at 19:41:21–19:42:21, then 194, then about 5
  for the rest of the row. Site a's home net shard (`e55d03dc`) was warm the whole time. Sites b and c held no shard
  for net 131375 until load reached them:
  - b's container started at 19:40:58 and c's at 19:41:02 (`tests/fanout/instcpu.py`).
  - Each did a full rehydrate. b took 122.6 s, ending 19:43:04, and resumed after a "Reduce your concurrent request
    rate (10058)" error at 19:41:01. c took 129.5 s, ending 19:43:11.
  - The pre-load `warm` keep never reached them: "shard warm 131375 site N: no answer in 25 s" for all three sites
    from 19:41:26 to 19:43:16.
  - **Cause: cold shards on b and c, still rehydrating.** It was not a catch-up deadline or a chain.
- **Why a cold c turned into receive-pack 503s.**
  - The edge's `validate_streamed` probes each non-last shard with `/catchup?wait=15&next=1`.
  - The home shard's catch-ups grew to 25.5 s at 19:41:40 and 73.5 s at 19:42:53. So the 15 s probe failed, and the
    pack went to the last shard, c, which was mid-rehydrate. c holds a `next`-less validate for 60 s, then answers
    503 warming.
  - The result: 400 receive-pack 503s, and validate p99 by minute of 75.8, 75.4, 48.9, 19.6 and 19.2 s (m60: 11.1,
    4.5, 5.4, 6.5 and 9.3).
  - About 820 pushes took over 15 s (m60: 39), and 28.5% of agent-seconds went to waiting there.
  - merge:only's 16 × 503 are the same warming answer, so B0-29 and B0-35's dispatch path goes through it too.
    testMerges waitMaxMs was 239,301 and inFlightMax 15 (m60: 4,496 and 1).
- **Why the home shard was slow.**
  - The home shard's container (`git#0#net:131375`) ran 2.66 to 2.81 vCPU per minute from 19:41 to 19:45. Its
    validate requests per minute were similar to m60's home shard: 2,491, 2,146, 2,213, 1,090 and 1,550, against
    1,910 to 2,416. m60's home ran at 0.85 to 1.52 vCPU.
  - The difference is catch-up work. These are the home shard's `shard catch-up stored` lines over the five minutes:

    | | catch-ups | entries ingested | packs | MB | index_ms total | ingest s |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | m61 `e55d03dc` | 878 | 2,348 | 10,121 | 466 | 263 s | 837 |
    | m60 `0c28e68a` | 266 | 816 | 7,518 | 371 | 158 s | 748 |

  - m61's log reached index 762 and m60's reached 465. So the m61 home shard ingested its entries about 3.1 times
    over, against 1.75 times on m60. Overlapping catch-up pages ingested the same entries again (`+next` catch-ups ran
    60 to 124 per minute) [inference from the counts; I did not trace which pages overlapped].
  - The first minute alone stored 4,673 packs and 207 MB, against m60's 2,138 and 104.
  - index_ms per pack averaged 106 ms (p90 139), against 18 to 21 ms on the pool shards of the same site.
  - In validate's stages, v_walk p99 grew from 390 to 4,775 ms over the row, and v_index p99 from 127 to 338 ms.
  - Lock waits were 0. **lock-split's `stockHoldMax` and writer section do not show in the shard logs.**
- **The `refSnap` stdin walk.** lock-split's `refSnap` change passes every ref's oid to `rev-list --stdin` instead of
  `--not --all`. On a local repository with 1,201 refs, 50 runs took 0.105 s against 0.099 s for `--not --all`. So it
  does not explain v_walk's growth [measured locally, small repository]. The walk's growth more likely comes from a
  CPU-saturated container [inference].
- **"no member answered": 0 on every site.** These are not the lookup starvation M4-WP4 found on B0-35 m57.

## 2. Tier sheds, 9,518 → 15,170

These are learner shed gates over the row, from memguard deltas (192's memguard failed from 19:41:50 to about
19:44:24, so its gaps lump into minute 4):

| gate | m60 | m61 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| late | 42,266 | 30,578 |
| readsMax | 4,126 | 10,873 |
| **rebuild** | **2,412** | **17,264** |
| answerBytes, forgot | about 1,800 | 2,136 |

- **The reads are not more numerous.** Learners took 109,566 reads (m60: 105,561), with the same query mix: packed
  69,322 / 65,795, core 27,921 / 25,364, lsRefs 12,323 / 14,401. Member /read per request was 2.22 against 2.26.
  Voters shed a few hundred reads in each row.
- **The same reads met a different gate.** `late` fell by 11.7k because rounds were faster: learner read-index
  rounds over 500 ms were 19 to 54% of reads by minute on m61, against 46 to 62% on m60. `rebuild` rose by 14.9k and
  readsMax by 6.7k.
- **The log moved twice as fast.** Entries appended per minute on m61 were 122, 107, 169, 152 and 83. On m60 they
  were 70, 63, 69, 70 and 58. Learners 193–195's rebuild sheds by minute followed: 156, 1,847, 3,711, 8,624 and 846
  (m60: 64, 403, 244, 517, 310). Their readsMax sheds rose in the same minutes, 517 and 982 in minutes 3 and 4.
- **Why a waiter sheds, from the code.**
  - A learner's whole-tier read that finds its dump stale and not yet due (`family::dump_wait`, once per
    LEARNER_DUMP_EVERY_MS, 500 ms) waits for the rebuild if that comes before its FOLLOWER_WAIT deadline
    (`member::waits_for_rebuild`).
  - After the wait, `dump_wait` is asked again, and it accepts only a dump whose key equals the tiers *now*
    (`dump_key`: each tier's applied index and row sum).
  - So when another entry applies during the wait, the first waiter to wake rebuilds, and every other waiter at a
    later state finds that dump stale and the next rebuild 500 ms away. That is past its deadline, so it sheds as
    `rebuild`.
  - Meanwhile every waiter holds one of the READS_MAX (256) slots, which is how readsMax sheds rose with them.
  - At roughly twice the entry rate, a wait is about twice as likely to straddle an apply [inference from the code
    and the per-minute counts; no trace row records a waiter's second shed].
- **Nothing in the read's contract needs the dump at the state now.** The read was granted at the leader's read
  index (`raft::ReadGrant::floor`). Any answer built at or after that floor is linearizable for it. The narrow
  answers already serve on exactly that rule (`family::narrow_hit`: `at >= floor`; "an answer built at index i by
  any member of the repository in this isolate is the answer every member gives at i"). The dump path is the one
  read cache that still demands equality with the current state.

## 4. CommitErrors holds at 19:44:51, 19:45:14 and 19:45:25

Site a held three times, each with 3 failed calls per follower out of 14 to 19:
- **Which calls failed.** AppendEntries that missed CALL_DEADLINE (1 s). The `missed` closure (`host.rs`) counts a
  Deadline failure, and AppendEnded still logs ok because the answer came late. No call errored.
- **Why they were late.** In the last 60 s, 46 calls took 1 s or more, against 21 in the middle minute. I split them
  with CallSent, CallArrived, CallLeft and AnswerArrived:
  - The follower's handler took p50 17 to 71 ms, and its storage gate on the reply had p50 41 ms left.
  - The leader's own storage confirm was open at send for 67% of the late calls, with p50 753 ms left, against 13%
    of fast calls.
  - The remainder, the wire plus the leader's output gate, was p50 600 ms.
  - Leader confirms took 3,000 to 6,500 ms per 10 s in the last minute (about 2,200 mid-row), with 2 or 3 over
    500 ms per 10 s, up to 1,753 ms. Lag rows: 7 in the whole row, so the loop itself was not lagging.
- **What loaded the leader then.** Its inbound `/raft`-coded requests went from about 40 to 480 per 10 s at t+240,
  about 2,556 from one sender. Site a's Workers Logs show a burst of single-entry
  `/entries?from=N&to=N&local=1&bytes=4000000` page reads on the leader from 19:44:20 (141, 83 and 131 per 20 s).
  These are the home shard's catch-up pages, the same over-ingesting catch-ups as in question 1. Learner hedges rose
  too [the link from page reads to the leader's confirm time is inference].
- **The `own_stall` gap.** `own_stall` excuses only a call whose confirm began at or before the send and ended after
  send + 1 s. Calls sent while a 189 to 833 ms confirm was open, which ended inside the deadline and still missed it,
  counted as failures. Examples: rounds 5269 and 5270 (189 ms of confirm left, calls of 1,686 and 1,895 ms). These
  holds did not move the leader; they cost the last 30 s of rounds.

## The ranked change

**1. A learner's whole-tier read is answered from a cached dump built at or above its grant's floor, not only from a
dump at the tiers' current state.**
- `dump_bytes` and `dump_wait` take the floor, as `narrow_bytes` and `narrow_wait` already do. The cache keeps the
  dump's core applied index with it and serves it when that index is at or above the floor and the metadata tier's
  loaded state matches.
- A waiter then never waits twice. One rebuild answers every read granted at or before its state. A read is shed as
  `rebuild` only when the newest dump is older than its floor and the next rebuild comes after its deadline.
- **Design first.** `design/DESIGN.md`, "Follower read", gets the sentence that a whole-tier answer may come from any
  state at or above the read's floor. The matching DECISIONS entry gets a flip: if any read returns a dump built
  below its floor, revert.
- **Tests and plants.**
  - Through `fold_answer`/`answer_after`: a read granted at floor f, with a dump cached at f+1 and the tiers then at
    f+2, is answered at once (it fails on main, which sheds it).
  - A read granted at f+3 against the same cache waits or sheds.
  - Plant 1: serve a dump below the floor. The second test catches it.
  - Plant 2: compare against the current state, as main does. The first test catches it.
- **Expected effect** [model guess, from m61's gate counts]:
  - Most of the 17,264 `rebuild` sheds and the readsMax sheds they fed (about 6.7k over m60) go away. Learners shed
    about 64k reads instead of 87.5k.
  - The edge's tier-shed 503s need every asked member to shed. Scaling them by the learners' shed share, that takes
    them from 15,170 to about 9,500 to 11,000, near m60's 9,518.
  - Writes ending 503 fall from 34.8% to about 22 to 25%.
  - Agent ok/s rises little from this alone, from 58.9 to about 63 to 66, because the larger loss of agent time on
    m61 was validate stalls.

**2.** Below it, for ok/s rather than the 503 share: warm b's and c's shards before load. Either the setup waits until
`warm` answers on every site, or a `next`-less validate on a shard that is rehydrating answers 503 with Retry-After
at once instead of holding 60 s. This removes about 486 warming 503s (1.1 points of the 34.8%) and most of the 28.5%
of agent-seconds spent in validate. If that time turned into pushes at m61's rate, agent ok/s would rise from 58.9
to about 70 [model guess].

**3.** Lower: stop catch-ups re-ingesting entries the shard already stored (3.1× on m61), which drove the home
container to 2.7 vCPU and the leader's page reads in the last minute. Before any change, a trace of which catch-up
pages overlap is needed.

**4.** `own_stall` excusing a call sent while any confirm is open is a small, separate fix. These holds cost rounds,
not 503s.
