## Deeper analysis

### 1. The catch-up tail, 23.0 / 26.3 s on second and hostile, is not in the gated line

miss503.py pools every network's miss lines. The 23.0 and 26.3 s catch-up p99s belong to the **second and hostile
networks' own stock whole clones** (`hot:clone*`), which are not B0-34 samples. The gated `read:cloneTtfb` probes clone
the monorepo in every phase, and the monorepo's catch-up waits are much shorter:

| Misses by network | j9 | kill | second | hostile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| monorepo: misses, catch-up waits, wait p99 | 86, 9, 8.2 s | 30, 7, 5.8 s | 41, 4, 4.6 s | 94, 35, 8.7 s |
| hot networks: misses, catch-up waits, wait p99 | | | 20, 15, 23.0 s | 52, 39, 26.3 s |

**The hot networks' 22–33 s waits are rehydrates.** Each hot network went hot cold, and every shard of it (sites a, b
and c, then a pool shard) rehydrated while its first clones waited. 22 rehydrates of 202 MB each
(`raw/shardev-mt7.jsonl.xz`, `shard rehydrate` lines):
- total 21.2 to 32.6 s; `markers` 1.4 to 3.0 s; `marked` 2.0 to 8.0 s; `tail` 14.2 to 25.5 s;
- `index_ms` 10.7 to 22.1 s and `spool_ms` 3.4 to 12.5 s over 8 packs, with `idx_used` 4 of 8;
- entries 0 to 3, so the lookup and `/entries` pages cost nothing (local pages answer in 2 to 232 ms).

So the time is `index-pack` on the four packs that have no stored `.idx` (a push's tee, `Stage`, stores none; the four
with one are promotions [inference: only a placed pack that carries an `.idx` gets one, `store::put_idx`]), plus the spool. Their 503s are the 18 "catch-up wait (cold)" and most of the 34 "at once".

**Does `disk-first-reads` fit a clone miss? No.**
- Its rule needs a disk that holds the named commit. A hot network's first clones arrive while every shard rehydrates,
  with no cursor and no disk: nothing to answer from.
- On the monorepo the wait is `stock: not a ref tip or behind HEAD` (`NOT_YET`) every time: the want is a tip the disk
  does not hold yet. The edge answers `ls-refs` from the group, so a clone's want is the group's newest tip by
  construction, which a lagging disk lacks. A commit already on the disk already composes with no catch-up (the
  composable path asks the container first, before any catch-up), which is the part of disk-first that applies.

**Why the monorepo's catch-ups ran 5 to 17.6 s.** 68 monorepo catch-ups ran over 5 s. Their time is outside every timed
lap: in the longest, the stored packs' ingest took 37 to 59 ms (`stored.total_ms`) and the rest came after the last
spool. 37 of the 40 catch-ups that misses waited on over 2 s carried inline packs only. What the untimed part is:
- **The multi-pack-index write [inference].** Every 50th ingest writes `git multi-pack-index write` under the writer's
  section, at nice 0. On the stored path it is timed: 20 writes, p50 4.6 s, p99 10.2 s. On `/ingest-batch` (inline
  packs) it runs the same way and reports nothing. `shardprocs` sampled a write with 7.62 CPU s on site a at 22:48:17,
  2.9 s into the 17.6 s catch-up that the j9 misses at 22:48:25 waited on (8.2 and 7.7 s). Over the row, 23 of 68 long
  catch-ups have a write sampled inside them against 289 of 2,089 short ones (34 % against 14 %; samples are 15 s apart).
- Other untimed parts are `make_room` (one `/repos` disk walk per catch-up), `/refs`, and the cursor puts. No field
  separates them, so the midx write is a hypothesis until timed.

Removing the monorepo misses' catch-up waits (each probe loses its miss's `caught_up_ms`, matched within 2.5 s) moves
clone TTFB p99 from 7.2 / 10.2 / 6.6 / 9.0 s to 5.7 / 10.2 / 6.6 / 5.4 s.

### 2. j9 got worse (7.2 s against 4.3 s), and it is not chain-wait

- **Chain-wait did not touch j9.** All 86 j9 misses went to site a, the home site, with `ready_ms` 0. None was forwarded,
  so `CHAIN_WAIT_FORWARDED` never applied.
- **The tail is a cluster at 22:48:25 to 22:48:29, the minute before the kill.** j9's p99 is 5.5 s over its first 240 s
  and 9.4 s from 240 s on. Its top four probes (9.4, 8.4, 7.8, 7.2 s) are misses that waited 7.7 to 8.2 s for the 17.6 s
  catch-up in (1), with the midx write in it.
- **mt6 had the same slow catch-ups later.** Its first long monorepo catch-up after the warm began at 314 s, in kill. In
  mt7 they began at 245 s, inside j9's window, as site a's first repack did (256 s against 428 s). The difference is
  when the shard's maintenance started relative to the phase boundary, not this build.

### 3. Human push on the edge clock, 4.3 to 5.4 s p99

The client and edge clocks now nearly agree (all phases: client p99 4.7 s, edge 4.6 s over 499 `ok` pushes), so the
gap is all on the edge. Split of the edge `server-timing` stages (`raw` a1 pushes file):

|---|---|---|---|
| front: token and install read, in parallel with the name resolve | 0.54 s | 1.69 s | 1.12 s |
| read gate | 0 | 1.20 s | 0.21 s |

- **Validate:** the container's own part is small (`v_index` p99 0.30 s, `v_walk` p99 0.41 s, `v_lock_wait` 0). The rest
  is waiting at the monorepo's site-a shard object, which ran 4,280 validates in the row at a wall p50 1.5 s and p99
  6.1 s.
- **Commit:** p99 2.0 s, the group's quorum write under journey 9's agent load.
- **Front:** the install `Authz` read and the name resolve each reach 1.5 to 3.5 s in the tail.
- No single stage reaches 3.3 s alone; the slowest pushes add a 1 to 2 s tail in three stages.

## The ranked change

**First: take the multi-pack-index write out of the writer's section, and time the catch-up's untimed parts.**
- **The change** (`containers/git/server/main.go`, `midxAfterIngest`): after every 50th ingest, start the write in the
  background under its own `TryLock` (as `startRepack` does) instead of inside the ingest's writer section. git writes
  the file beside it and renames it in, so a reader sees the old index or the new one, and packs ingested meanwhile are
  read as before. `/ingest-batch` reports `midx_ms` as `/ingest` does, and `catch_up` laps `make_room`, the ingest and
  `/refs` separately, so the next row confirms or rules out the hypothesis.
- **Expected effect on clone TTFB p99** if the midx write is the untimed part: about 5.7 / 10.2 / 6.6 / 5.4 s, from 7.2 /
  10.2 / 6.6 / 9.0 s. Kill stays at 10.2 s: its tail is the kill path (site a torn down, site b's 2 s chain bound, then
  site c's 5.1 to 6.8 s chain wait), which `G28` (the builder installing the R2 chain head) addresses.
- **What it does not reach:** hits alone are p99 3.4 to 5.2 s through the orb (A33), so no phase gets near 1.1 s on the
  client clock. The hot networks' rehydrates are not in the gated line; a stored `.idx` for pushed packs would take out most
  of their `index_ms`, 11 to 22 s [inference].
