The timing fits: b went back to `tail` (15) at 14:39, the minute site a's shard came back and put a new `head` to both buckets. So b composes because the chain froze with the builder, not because it holds no chain. Site c holds the same frozen

second's composed-on-shard p50 / p99 3.10 / 6.14 s, against `composed: r2` 2.62 / 5.05 s, and they put 8 of second's 9
misses over 5 s.

**Proposal (not built):** builder failover. While the home site's first shard cannot step (rehydrating after a kill,
or `warming`), the first running shard on the other chain site steps the chain and brings the buckets up, as the
builder does. Shape: `builder` becomes "the first shard of the first chain site, in `chain_buckets` order, whose shard
is caught up". The home keeps the role whenever it can, so there is one builder in steady state. A short-lived second
builder during a handover writes the same content-addressed files, and `head` is last-writer-wins on a chain that only
moves forward. Test: with chain [a, c], a cold home shard and c caught up, c steps after its catch-up; with a warm home,
only a steps. Expected effect on mt14 [inference]: the chain keeps stepping from c through 14:32–14:39, b's and c's
misses stay `tail` from R2, and second's 8 shard composes over 5 s become R2 tails. Before building it, check the
alternative of raising `tailMax`. A larger tail is held whole in the shard object and the edge, and `hit-gate`'s
streaming caps the edge's copy at about 4 KB, so the shard object's copy is the constraint.
