## Blocked on (2026-11-10 02:00Z), and how each is routed around

1. **Member SQLite over 4 GiB cannot be read.** workers-rs 0.8.7's `SqlStorage::database_size()` is
   `self.inner.database_size() as usize`, and `usize` is 32 bits on wasm32, so every reading saturates at
   4,294,967,295 (`tests/m2/results/m2-wp1.md`, "Ceiling", saw the same). The probe of `repo:196627` read exactly that
   on all three members. B0-30's line is 8 GiB, so the gate quantity is unreadable through the binding. **Route:** the
   site reads `ctx.storage.sql.databaseSize` as an `f64` through `js_sys::Reflect` (test routes only: `/fold
   {"log"}` and `/bench_seed`), shipped in this branch's site day.
2. **Nothing counts `SQLITE_FULL`.** A failed storage write resets the member (`Host::fail`) and logs "storage write
   failed". **Route:** members count writes that fail with `SQLITE_FULL` or "database or disk is full" in `/stats`
   (`sqliteFull`, per isolate), and the monitor also queries Workers Logs of all four site scripts for both strings
   every hour, which survives isolate restarts.
3. **The existing 4 GB groups are not usable.** `repo:196628` (BF-20's 3.99 GB tier) had no leader at 01:14Z (sites 0
   and 1 did not answer `/status`, site 2 a follower at term 18), and its repository answers 503 "not leader".
   `repo:196627`'s members are already past the 4 GiB reading ceiling (about 6.6 GB by M2's per-row rate). Both are
   owned by the BF-20 seed accounts, not by `m3org`. **Route:** a fresh `m3org/b0-30` repository, seeded on all three
   members through `/bench_seed` (the comment-row seed BF-20 used; `{"load"}` is the push load, not the seed).
4. **Push rate.** One network validates about 2 pushes/s (`tests/am1/results/am1-runs.md`, "Diagnosis"), and journey
   9 needs 4.17/s. **Route:** as the brief allows, see "Substitutions" (to be filled in when the run starts).

### Update 2026-11-10 09:00Z: what happened to each route, and two more blockers

- Routes 1 to 3 are built and shipped (commits `7bb30e0`, `724f954`): `/bench_seed {"b030": {"size": true}}` reads
  `databaseSize` as an f64, `sqliteFull` counts space failures, and `m3org/b0-30` (`repo:1048586`, created through
  Authz `CreateRepo` because REST create needs 32.13, which graft-m1 does not enable) was seeded to 1,340,000 comment
  rows of 1,800 B on all three members: **4,017,184,768 B each** at the end of the seed (07:5xZ).
- Route 4 is no longer needed for validation: `origin/main` `1e25da8` (merged as `8d73e9c`) has `fanout`. But see 5.
5. **Every edge route that reads the repository's tiers dumps the 4 GB tier.** `workers/edge/src/api.rs`,
   `tier_rows`: "The repository's `{"rows": true}` read on its leader", which `workers/site/src/family.rs`, `read_json`,
   answers with "both tiers' rows at this index, at most `graftyard_api::hosted::ROWS_MAX`" (50,000). Its callers include
   agent and App pushes (`workers/edge/src/agents.rs`, `push_context`: `let rows = match
   crate::api::tier_rows(c, repo, None).await`), the task and lease API, and GraphQL and REST repository reads. On the
   4 GB tier one such `/read` used about 302 s of CPU and reset the leader (08:03Z and 08:37Z); the 4 GB members took
   about 5 min to answer again each time. This is a defect for any hot group, not only this row (left as found: no
   design or edge edits here). **Route:** no agent-class pushes and no live edge commands on the 4 GB group; pushes
   are m3-admin's (Human class: `push_context` returns before the read); agent rows are recorded by the leader in
   batched entries (`/bench_seed {"record"}`), see "Substitutions".
6. **The first archive took the leader's isolate down.** With a test cap (`/bench_seed {"archiveCap": {"max":
   100000000, "range": 2097152}}`) the leader archived 1..1 and 2..39 at 08:50Z, then logged "Cannot invoke closure
   from previous WASM instance" and the group sat leaderless at term 7 (applied 332/331/331). After an archive,
   `Host::truncate` calls `crate::load::set_source`, whose id-block check read **every** log row above the archive into
   memory (`SELECT row FROM log WHERE idx > ?`): 120 MB here, and up to `LOG_MAX_BYTES` (2 GiB) in the design. **Fix**
   (`c3a2aa9`, site code, test and production builds): the check reads only rows under 4 KB, since an `IdBlock` entry
   carries no events. Shipped by site day 5.
7. **The hosted archive alarm drains about 0.2 MB/s, and only through a warm shard.** At journey 9's 2.48 GB of
   inline packs a simulated day the log took in about 0.8 MB/s (1.6 pushes/s x 323 KB, plus about 0.03 MB/s of
   metadata rows). From 09:57Z, when the log passed `LOG_MAX_BYTES`, the alarm archived one `ARCHIVE_RANGE_MAX`
   (2 MiB) range about every 4 s: a promote through the leader's site's git cache shard (about 2.3 s), then the
   two-bucket put and `Archived` (about 2 s), each its own entry. At 10:20Z the log was at 2.75 GB and rising. Two
   things made it worse:
   - **Cold shards.** Each leader change (`STEP_DOWN` on `CommitErrors`, about every 5 to 15 min under this load) moved
     promotion to the new leader's site's shard. Shards b and c had never caught up on this network, and their
     `/catchup`, `/promote` and `/merge` ran 60 to 430 s and ended `exceededMemory` (Workers Logs, 09:58 to 10:18Z).
     The alarm's runs then timed out (`RUN_FOR`, 120 s), and archiving stopped even with the load paused (10:22 to
     10:27Z). With leadership moved back to site 0 (`/transfer`), it resumed at about 16 ranges a minute.
   - **A larger test range ran out of memory.** `archiveCap {range: 8 MiB}` (10:11Z, test isolates only) took a
     member's isolate past its memory limit at 10:14Z ("archive: Error: Durable Object's isolate exceeded its memory
     limit and was reset"). Cleared at 10:16Z; every member is back on the design's 2 GiB and 2 MiB.

   **Route:** (a) the load keeps the leader on site 0: once a minute, a leader elsewhere transfers to site 0 when site 0
   is caught up (`tests/am1/b030/load.mjs`, `keepHome`); (b) from 10:33Z the inline pack bytes are 0.31 GB a simulated
   day (40 KB a push at 1.6/s, journey 9's mean pack size) instead of 2.48, so the alarm can bring the log back to its
   cap and hold it there. The metadata rows a day are unchanged. See "Substitutions".
8. **A shard far behind cannot catch up.** At 10:38Z pushes and archiving stopped together. Site a's git cache shard
   (`git#0#net:1048586`) restarted (its isolate went over memory on `/validate`) and resumed from its saved
   `IngestCursor` at index 1,663, about 4,480 entries behind the leader. `ShardHost::catch_up`
   (`crates/git/src/host.rs`) reads the whole range behind the cursor (`log.entries(chain, range.start, range.end)`),
   holds every entry's inline packs (about 1.4 GB here), and only then ingests and saves the cursor. Each attempt
   paged `/entries` from 1,663 to about 1,767 (about 100 pages of 300 KB entries), then ended `exceededMemory` or
   "Promise will never complete", from the same cursor every time (10:38 to 10:58Z). Every route that catches up first
   (`/validate`, `/promote`, `/merge`, `/catchup`) failed the same way. The leader also ran out of memory twice under
   these calls and lost leadership; it came back after one `/campaign`. **Fix** (site code, `graftyard-git`
   `ShardHost::catch_up`): catch up in pieces of `CATCH_UP_ENTRIES` (32) entries. Each piece is ingested, has its
   ref changes applied, and saves the cursor before the next piece is read, so memory is bounded by one piece and a
   failed attempt resumes past the pieces it finished. Shipped by site day 6. This is a defect for any shard that falls
   behind on a hot group, not only this run.
9. **A shard with no cursor cannot rehydrate a hot group.** After site day 6 the first shard caught up in pieces to
   index 6,172 (11:31Z) and archiving resumed at about 24 ranges a minute. But the network's `fanout` k was 2, so
   about half the pushes went to `git#0#pool:2`, which had never held the network. Its `ShardHost::rehydrate` reads the
   whole tail above the last `Promoted` (`log.entries(chain, from, head.applied)`), about 2.5 GB of inline packs here,
   and ran out of memory on every attempt ("fanout: git#0#pool:2: Error: Durable Object's isolate exceeded its memory
   limit and was reset.; validating on the first shard"), then fell back to the first shard after about 130 s each
   time. Setting k to 1 (`/bench_seed {"fanout": 1}`, 11:23Z) did not hold: the leader's load reports stepped it back
   to 2. **Fix** (`graftyard-git` `ShardHost::rehydrate`): the marked packs are ingested in batches of
   `INGEST_BATCH_BYTES`, and the tail is read, fetched and ingested in pieces of `CATCH_UP_ENTRIES`. Shipped by site
   day 7.
10. **The push path still stalls in the git cache shard.** After site day 7 (11:50Z) the first shard caught up and
    archiving resumed. But every leader change or shard reset sent a cold shard through a rehydrate from the last
    `Promoted` (index 1,483, about 5,400 entries and 2.4 GB below the head). In pieces it no longer ran out of memory,
    but it took longer than a push's 180 s client deadline and the archive run's `RUN_FOR` (120 s), which both wait on it.
    Pushes timed out (`http 0` at 180 s), `PrSync`'s test merges answered 409 ("commit … is not here") for the whole
    rehydrate, and archiving made no progress from 11:55Z. `fanout` stepped k from 2 to 4 under the stalled load, which
    added more cold pool shards. **Route:** from 12:15Z the load sends no pushes (`B030_PUSHES=0`). The metadata rows a
    simulated day are unchanged, recorded by the leader. The log then takes only those entries, about 0.14 GB a
    simulated day, and the alarm archives them once the shard serves `/promote` again. This drops journey 9's
    inline-pack bytes from simulated day 1.34 on. The push path's defects (5, 7 to 10) are recorded here for the design.

## Substitutions

Journey 9 as M0 replayed it (`tests/m0/results/m0-wp9.md`, B0-30) is 30 simulated days of 74.4 GB of inline agent
packs plus agent metadata rows. The hosted run keeps the bytes a simulated day and the metadata rows a simulated day,
and changes how they arrive (`tests/am1/b030/load.mjs`, header):

| What | Journey 9 (M0) | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | 30 days | 30 simulated days in 40 h (18x) | the brief's 40 h |
| Pushes | 4.17/s agent pushes, 10 to 70 KB | 1.6/s Human pushes to `refs/heads/b030/<i>` (500 branches): to sim day 0.938 (09:09 to 10:22Z) 322,917 B each, the same 2.48 GB a simulated day; from 10:33Z 40,365 B each, 0.31 GB a simulated day (blocker 7) | 4.17/s x 18 = 75/s is not reachable; agent-class pushes hit blocker 5. 1.6/s stays under `hot`'s 2/s so packs stay inline. Each blob is a fixed base with 64 random bytes, so repacks delta them |
| Tasks and PRs | 6,050 a day via the task API | 6,050 a day recorded by the leader: `TaskCreated` (900 B summary), `PrOpened` (1.8 KB body) on a pushed branch, a 16 KB `PathSet`; `PrClosed` 20 simulated minutes later | blocker 5 |
| Statuses | 48,400 a day | 48,400 a day, 220 B description, recorded | blocker 5 |
| Provenance | 60,500 a day | 60,500 a day, recorded | blocker 5 |
| Batching | one command each | one entry of up to 40 tasks, 400 statuses, 500 provenance rows every 10 s | about 10 M separate commands would not fit 40 h |
| Request retention | 48 h | `GRAFTYARD_REPO_REQUEST_RETENTION_S=9600` (48 h / 18), repository chains only | at 48 h the `request` table would hold the whole run |

The metadata rows a day are journey 9's. The log bytes a day were journey 9's for the first 0.94 simulated days and
about an eighth of them after (blocker 7); the cap still binds, because the log starts the reduced phase at about
2.6 GB, above `LOG_MAX_BYTES`, and the alarm holds it at the cap from there. What the reduction does not exercise is
2.48 GB a simulated day of archive churn on the member's free pages; M0 measured that on one object. The number of git
pushes and the command count are not journey 9's. B0-30 measures storage, not push throughput.
