- **Read-beat did what it claimed:** each entry now goes to each voter about once, and the entry bytes to the followers are back at m27's. The read rounds still happen, about 30 calls/s to each follower, but they are empty AppendEntries at `matched`.
- **It did not bring commit p99 back.** Entries cut per second rose only from 3.8 to 4.3 (m27 4.8), and `queue:slot` p99 is still 3.2 s against m27's 0.46 s. Round, confirm and both append legs got only a little faster. `append:2` p99 is unchanged at 708 ms. So the re-sent entries were not what made each cycle slow [inference: the cycle's stages moved by 10 to 20% while the repeated bytes fell by 85%].
- **`/stats.clock` (operator's request).** At the end of the row `suspect` was false and the window was 8 zeros, because the load had stopped. Over the boot, 143,238 of 147,133 stamps (97.4%) fell in the lag buckets of 256 ms and up (bucket 9 and up; 55.9% at 512 ms and up), `lagMax` was 3,520 ms, and `leaseFallbacks` was 5,061 (reads the leader granted while it was suspect). So the leader was suspect for most of the load, as run 2's replay inferred, and that inference is now measured. The trace's proxy (a `/raft` call's `x-graft-sent` stamp minus the leader's trace clock at `CallArrived`, which is not the guard's own reading) gives p50 237 / p99 2,463 ms for learner calls and p50 561 / p99 3,302 ms for follower calls (run 2: 213 / 3,082 and 420 / 3,247).
- **What still differs from m27:** `deps:*` p99 (582 against 222), `/outcome` asks (256 against 7), learner holds (22,808 calls held 500 ms; F85 addresses these), and inbound `/raft` (90,856 against 54,395). The extra `/raft` is the read rounds and learner read-index calls, which read-beat made cheap but did not remove.

Next is F85 (learner holds; `f85-hold`) on the next B0-29, and then the question of why the leader's clock lags 250 ms or more (lease-clock's guard) under this load at all, since that is what forces a round for every read.
