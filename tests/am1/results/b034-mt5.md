## The 43 to 88 s waits on the second and hostile networks

Thirteen catch-ups on `repo:3473411` and `3538953` to `3538955` waited 20 to 88 s for the lock alone, ten of them over
40 s. Each one:
- began just after that shard's rehydrate ended (of the ten over 40 s, seven within 1 s, one 2.7 s, two 40 to 47 s
  after it);
- overlapped one or two `/upload-pack`s that answered `stock: not composable`, took the lock shared once their catch-up
  ended (`arrived + caught_up_ms` 0.1 to 32 s), and ran 36 to 119 s; the wait ended within 5 s of one of their ends in
  nine of the thirteen.

Those readers are a1's hot clones of the second and hostile networks (`hot:cloneTtfb`): a whole clone with no
`deepen 1` and no `ofs-delta`, so `composable` refuses it and stock `upload-pack` streams git/git's 0.3 GB to the
client through the orb's proxy. a1 aborts at 120 s, which is the 118 to 119 s walls ("canceled" in Workers Logs). The
container holds the network's lock shared for as long as the stock answer streams (`upload`, main.go, "A stock answer
holds the network's lock shared only while its client reads"). `clientWriteTimeout` (30 s, compose.go) bounds one
stalled write, not a slow reader, and `stockMax` (2) bounds how many run, not how long.

**So a hostile client can hold it.** Any caller that fetches a stock pack and reads it slowly holds the network's
shared lock until it finishes or stops reading for 30 s, and every catch-up of that network waits behind it, with every
new validate queued behind the waiting writer. Two such readers per container are enough. `lock-split` takes that away
for additive writers: a catch-up's ingest and `/refs` no longer wait for readers. Only the deleters (evict, which never
waits, and fresh init) and a D/F `/refs` would still queue behind a slow reader.
