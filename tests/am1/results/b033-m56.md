## The orchestrator's four questions

### 1. Why learners answer late

A learner's read-index read is its round to the leader (`IndexSent` 1 to `IndexEnded` 2), then the wait for its own
applied index to reach the read index (`ResStepped` 3 to the first `Applied` 4 at or above it), then any rebuild wait.
`late` is FOLLOWER_WAIT (1 s) passing before the answer (`member.rs`, `read_wait`).

| learner | site | round p50 / p90 | rounds ≥ 1 s | apply wait p50 / p90 | apply waits ≥ 1 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 192 | a (enam) | 208 / 1,000 | 322 of 2,740 | 0 / 0 | 2 of 1,360 |
| 195 | a (enam) | 220 / 1,000 | 324 of 2,953 | 0 / 0 | 2 of 1,527 |
| 193 | b (weur) | 293 / 1,000 | 320 of 2,691 | 0 / 80 | 4 of 1,524 |
| 194 | c (apac) | 512 / 1,287 | 736 of 2,829 | 493 / 2,530 | 796 of 2,313 |

- **On the near learners (a, b) the round runs past FOLLOWER_WAIT.** The leader answers a `ReadIndex` in the same
  input it takes it (`IndexTaken` 5 to `ResSent` 6: p50 0, p90 0, n 7,220). The time is in the reply's way back: on
  calls of 900 ms or more, the learner's fetch left at once (out p50 96 to 179 ms) and the answer took 1,137 to 1,256
  ms from the leader's handler return to the learner (`AnswerArrived` 21's `x-graft-left`). On 83 to 89% of those slow
  calls a leader storage confirm was still open at that return (rest p50 100 to 119 ms), against 56 to 60% on fast calls:
  the reply rides behind the leader's output gate, then the wire. The learners' own loop lag was 0 and their open
  fetches 7 or 8 (p90 17), so the learners are not the hold.
- **On the far learner (194, apac) the apply wait runs past it too.** Its applied index trails the leader's read
  index: p50 493 ms, p90 2.5 s, 796 waits of 1 s or more. Its rounds are slower out (p50 688 ms on slow calls). It shed
  20,698 `late` of the 35,317.
- Rebuild is not the late cause: a read whose rebuild is not due by its deadline is counted `rebuild`, not `late`.
- Learner read-index rounds slow as /read rises: p50 83 ms at 126 /read/s (first 30 s), 400 to 527 ms at 466 to 578
  /read/s (from +150 s), with 20 to 30% of rounds at 1 s or more.

### 2. Whether writes' shed retries feed the load

- **Offered tier reads:** a1 offered 166 requests/s (side commands 127 /s, agent pushes 41.6 /s, fast-user 1.7 /s). Each
  needs one tier read (rows plus ranges; a push's gate reads access and agent rows in one, `read_gate`).
- **Learner /read rose 4.6×** with offered load flat: all members' edge-originated /read per second by minute 189, 285,
  445, 563, 581; learners carry 98% (the voters 2 to 6 /s each). Query mix at the learners: packed 80,620, core 25,551,
  lsRefs 14,576.
- **Reads per write:** 123,769 /reads reached members over 300 s against about 49,800 offered requests (2.5 per request
  over the row; 3.5 per request in the last two minutes). A write's tier read under SHED_RETRY_FOR (4 s) walks the 4
  learners plus 2 followers per pass, with backoff 125 to 2,000 ms: a 503 after 4 s is two to four passes, 6 to 18
  member reads, and those writes are where the wall p50 of 5.5 s comes from. [inference: the edge does not log passes
  per request; the per-minute reads against offered requests is measured]
- So yes: the shed retries are most of the learners' load growth, and the load slows their rounds (1 above), which
  makes more `late`, which makes more retries.

## Ranked fix (code-only)

1. **The write path's tier read should not retry the learners it just heard shed; it should wait on one in-flight
   read per key.** 90% of the 503s and the budget behind 2,551 `full` pushes are one cause: a write's tier read (rows
   plus ranges, the same query for every write to the repository) is asked by each write separately, and each shed
   pass is asked again within SHED_RETRY_FOR, so the learners' /read grew 4.6× while offered load stayed flat, which
   slowed their rounds behind the leader's output gate and made more `late`. edge-read-batch already shares a
   write-class identity read of one key with the next read; the same share for a write's tier read (`core_rows_q`
   keyed by chain, query and the leader's applied index it must cover) cuts the reads to one per isolate per pass. The
   gate is not ANSWER_BYTES_MAX (2,012 of 56,938) or the dump rebuild alone (8,881), so this is learner load, not
   admission. Second, the far learner's apply wait (194) is a placement question (a far learner serving read-index
   reads), not a code one.

Not ranked: the cold shards (4) are a warm-up gap on fresh networks; the StepDown samples show no transfer to fix on
this row.
