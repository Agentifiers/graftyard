### The cause: a second edge colo made SUSPECT's withhold rule fire

The voters' rule (`raft::SUSPECT` = 2 colos in 10 s): a follower stops promising its leader when edge reports from 2
distinct colos arrive within 10 s while the leader has made no progress for it for `CALL_DEADLINE`. Once the promise
lapses, one report starts a campaign.

- **Colos** (`tests/fanout/colos.py`, the voters' `Reported` rows): m53's reports came from LAX 17,274 and **DFW 8,995**
  (and site scripts 644, never counted). m50, m51 and m52 came from **LAX alone** (2,312, 7,290 and 9,344 reports). The
  edge's own logs agree: at 21:50:00Z, 1,079 requests from LAX and 576 from DFW. At 14:46:00Z (m52), 1,549 from LAX and
  none from DFW.
- **The rule's firing** (`tests/fanout/withhold.py`): follower-seconds with a late leader and reports from 2 or more
  colos numbered **0 on m50 and m52, and 79 on m53**. A late leader with reports from only 1 colo, which cannot withhold,
  numbered 66 on m50, 203 on m52 and 13 on m53. **12 of m53's 16 leader changes in the load window followed such a
  second within 20 s.**
- So m52's leader was late for its followers more often than m53's (203 against 92 follower-seconds), and it kept the
  leadership only because every report came from one colo [measured]. B0-33's "zero leader changes" line has passed or
  nearly passed on m50 to m52 because the client sat behind one colo, not because the leader was healthy [inference].
- **Where DFW came from** [inference]. The orb reaches the edge by anycast, and this run's `/etc/hosts` pin went to an
  address the edge's name resolved to. Probing after the run: 20 requests to that address landed LAX 13 and DFW 7, and
  20 to m52's pinned address landed LAX 16 and DFW 4. On m52 the same pin landed LAX only. The build did not choose the
  colo.
- **The six `StepDown` alarms** (Workers Logs, `member alarm:`): 5 × `CommitErrors` and 1 × `Confirm` (site a 21:49:13,
  p99 1,238, 6 of 7 slow). No `AppendEnded` row in any trip window failed, so every counted failure was a deadline miss.
  The leader's deadline misses fell from 35.8% of calls on m52 to 10.6%. Each trip came 7 to 17 s after a leader change, while the new leader's followers still served the walks and outcomes
  of the change before. That is the symptom the orchestrator named.
- **The first change** (21:48:27Z, term 2 to 4) was a real stall: site a's leader had a 5,044 ms confirm ending at
  21:48:21.8 and loop lags of 2.4 and 1.5 s. Reports from both colos then withheld, and members 0 and 1 started

### The `ls-refs` split

`ls_refs` at the edge (`workers/edge/src/lib.rs`) makes one `read_off_leader` pass: learners first, then followers, with
no retry and never the leader. Any read that comes back without a value goes on to the shard. So the 3,441 that went to
the shard were **declined by the members, shed rather than failed**. The learners were at `READS_MAX` or waiting past
`FOLLOWER_WAIT` on read indexes from a changing leader [measured, above]; that the followers shed rather than failed is an
inference, since no follower answer is logged per read. The shard then
answered **503** to all of them (edge 503 on upload-pack: 3,441, wall p50 2.9 s, p99 6.5 s). The shard's own reason is
not in the logs: the edge's error line carries no body. On m52 the same path sent 1,768 to the shard, all 503 as well.

**Ranked candidates** (most likely first, each with the test that settles it):

1. **Whole-tier dumps answered on the acting leader's thread.** Two paths reach it: (a) read-index `packed`
   reads that a `read_spread` pass sends to a voter it takes for a follower (the pass leaves out only the leader its
   isolate has cached, so a stale cache after a change lands on the new leader, which serves it), and (b) the `Spread::Leader` verdict and
   `call_opt`'s walk, which send the same body to the leader with no `index` when every member asked declined it as
   not theirs (`notLeader`, a 4xx, or none asked). DESIGN says whole-tier reads are "never on to the leader once one
   sheds them" ("Follower read" and the 500-agent load paragraph); a decline is not a shed, so (b) is within the text
   but against its intent after a change. Evidence: 3,835 dump leader reads and 1,625 dump read-index reads on the leader in
   m53 (none of the first kind on m50 or m52), the two dump bursts after changes, and the two stalls above. Each
   rebuild after a tier move costs a whole `TierRows::dump`; a cache hit is free, so the cost scales with tier moves
   × readers, which is highest right after a change. **Test**: count dump rebuilds on the leader (`dump_builds`,
   already counted, into `/stats` and memguard) against its tick lag per second on the next row; and replay the
   decision in `spread_verdict` on m53's trace to count reads that would have gone to the leader. **Fix sketch, not
   built**: a whole-tier read never goes to the leader. `Spread::Leader` for a `packed` query becomes a
   503 with `Retry-After` (the edge's existing `read_failed`), and a member that leads answers a read-index whole-tier
   read `notLeader` instead of serving it. That is a DESIGN "Follower read" edit first; edge and site code, not
   `crates/raft`.
2. **The callee's queue on the out-leg.** 53 of 140 slow m53 trips met a follower with ≥ 20 requests open at entry or
   a handler ≥ 500 ms, at callee inbound p90 774/s against 225/s for fast trips. That inbound is the walk, outcome and
   `/suspect` traffic changes create, which candidate 1's fix and fewer changes both shrink. **Test**: the same split
   after candidate 1, with the callee's `/read` share at entry.
3. **The leader's output gate behind its own confirm.** On m52 this was the largest named cause (137 of 298 slow
   trips); on m53 21 of 140, because `arrival=mem` and cut 2 shortened the transactions. A 1 s confirm still holds
   every send queued behind it. **Test**: none new; it stays in the split above. A lever exists only if 1 and 2 do not
   take the tail under 1 s.
4. **Unexplained: 66 slow m53 trips with neither** a gate nor a busy callee: the out-leg p50 ~1 s with the callee idle
   at entry and no open confirm at either end. Candidates: the platform's DO-to-DO queue and the leader's own
   microtask backlog before `fetch` is issued (`AppendQueued` → `AppendIssued` is 0 ms in every row, so the fetch call
   itself is not it). **Test**: compare against `CallSent`'s open-fetches column per trip; if the count of in-flight
   subrequests from the leader explains them (Workers' 6 simultaneous open connections per isolate), it is a
   connection-pool queue and joins candidate 1's fix.

The learners at `READS_MAX` with read-index p99 5.7 s follow from 1 and 2: their read-index calls wait on the same
leader that is serving dumps.

**What goes first, if approved**: the measurement for candidate 1 (dump rebuilds per second on the leader in
memguard, and the `spread_verdict` replay on m53), code-only and test-faults-only, then the DESIGN edit and the fix.
Nothing in `crates/raft`.

## Candidate (1) built: `tier-off-leader` (code-only, branch `tier-off-leader`)

The orchestrator's decision: build candidate (1) with its measurement folded in. Candidates (2) to (4) wait for the
first hosted row after it ships.

- **Rule** (`design/DESIGN.md`, "Follower read"; `decisions/DECISIONS.md`, "`tier-off-leader`"): a whole-tier dump or
  an `ls-refs` never runs on the acting leader, whether others shed or declined it, unless the group is `Solo`. One
  predicate, `graftyard_lookup::leader_never_serves`, used by the member's `gate` (a leader answers such a read-index
  read `notLeader`, naming itself) and by `spread_verdict` (a declined one is shed when any member named a leader or
  term, and `Absent` only when none is joined).
- **Replay on m53** (`tests/fanout/tieroff.py`): of 5,460 dumps on the acting leader, 1,625 would have been refused and
  moved to the pass's next member, and 3,835 never sent (the upper bound on reads the rule turns into 503s or shed
  retries). m50: 452 refused, 0 never sent. m52: 971 and 0. No `ls-refs` reached a voter as leader in any of them.
- **Instruments**: `/stats` `leaderRefused` (summary and full), and `memguard.mjs` records `dumpBuilds` and
  `leaderRefused` per voter per sample; per second is the difference between samples.
- **Tests**: `a_follower_serves_only_its_reads_under_the_caps_and_never_stale` (site) and
  `a_whole_tier_read_every_member_declines_never_goes_to_the_leader` (lookup), both failing on main;
  `tests/tieroff/plant.py`, 7 of 7 caught.
- **The flip to watch**: the API-read served share of the next B0-29 or B0-33 row (under 89.1%, or run 8 below run 7's
  100% with `leaderRefused` above zero).
- **The flip, split (2026-11-19, branch `b033-tieroff`).** The orchestrator kept 89.1% on B0-29's `read:api` and set
  B0-33's half against m53's REST and GraphQL served share, 29.1% (`tests/fanout/apishare.py`; m52 47.6%). The edge now
  logs each 503 this rule makes as `{"shed": "tierOff"}` (`spread_verdict`'s `TierOff`, `api::read_failed`), and
  `tests/fanout/tieroff503.py` counts them against the row's 503s. B0-33's half fires only if those are a large share,
  and only when the row's leader changes and colos compare with m53's; else it is reported and left undecided.
  `tests/fanout/confirmstmts.py` splits the leader's confirms by statements per flush (m52 900+: p50 545, p99 2,825
  ms; m53: none).
