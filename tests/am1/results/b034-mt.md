### The flip: did a catch-up wait over 1 s for the exclusive lock behind stock readers?

**Yes, once: site c, 13.0 s (corrected 2026-11-19; this section first said no).** The exclusive section is `lock_ms`
(from `l.Lock()` returning to unlock, `indexIngest`); every one over 1 s is the midx write (`lock_ms − midx_ms` at most
22 / 39 / 12 ms on sites a / b / c). The waits are elsewhere: `total_ms − index_ms` is `/ingest`'s time before
`indexIngest`, which is `handleIngest` taking the lock alone to `adopt`. Four stored catch-ups waited over 10 s there
(`tests/fanout/lateingest.py`, with the `shardprocs` sampler's clock moved onto Workers Logs' by the median gap to its
`/status` events, 9,228 ms):

| Site | Wait (s from T0) | Validates in the lock | Last validate ends | Stock reads computing in the lock | Stock processes in samples | Behind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | 535.6 → 559.3 (23.7 s) | 12 | 545.8 s | 0 | 2, 1 | validates and a `/merge`, then stock |
| a | 696.8 → 717.5 (20.7 s) | 10 | 718.8 s | 0 | 2 | validates and two `/merge`s |
| a | 734.1 → 748.6 (14.5 s) | 16 | 741.3 s | 0 | 1 | validates and a `/merge` |
| c | 641.3 → 654.3 (13.0 s) | **0** | – | **2** (caught up 616.0 s; first byte 642.5 and 652.8 s, over the cap) | 1 | **stock readers only** |

Site c's wait had no validate, merge or catch-up in it, only two stock reads computing their packs, so it waited behind
stock readers for 13.0 s: the flip's condition. On site a stock processes were running too, but every wait ended at or
after its last validate, so they are the validates' and merges' waits, with stock at most a share.

The two earlier counts disagreed because they measured different things: "1–2 stock fetches open" counted `shardprocs`
samples in a widened window without moving the sampler's clock, and "two had none" came from `attr.py` ending a stock
reader's lock at its headers (`headers_ms`), which for a stock answer comes before the pack is computed. `attr.py` now
ends it at the first byte (`first_ms`). Neither counted site c, the one wait behind stock alone.

What it means: option 4 (stock without the network lock) has met its flip on one 13 s wait. The larger effect on site
c was not the flip but the stalled reader below, which held the lock for 15 minutes; the write deadline removes that.
Option 4 stays unbuilt until the next row shows whether waits behind live stock readers remain once stalled ones are
gone (`decisions/DECISIONS.md`, `b034-tails`, option 4).

### The G26 finding: a chain with no `Settings` row still gets the first ref

The monorepo's `ranges` read answers `"default_branch": null` (read at 20:52Z): `b034setup.mjs` with `B034_CREATE=owner`
makes the repository by `CreateRepo` and `RepoOwner` only, so it has no `Settings` row, and `family.rs` answers null
"before the first `Settings`". The shard then sends no `head=`, and the container keeps the old rule, `refs/heads/main`
when it exists, else the page's first ref (`handleRefs`). So G26 did nothing for this network, and m1b34's failure
repeated:
- **Before site a's post-kill rehydrates ended (512 s):** 424 of 437 miss lines composed (97%), 0 over the cap.
- **After:** 1 of 25 composed; 15 lines named the cap (4 answered stock, 11 a 503 within the deadline). Site a's
  rehydrates ran 309 → 513 s, 440 → 632 s and 701 → 860 s, and the builder put a 20 KB base at 20:34:40Z (513 s),
  `base-e6f3565c…`, a commit in no linux history and not in the seed plan's 600 future tips: a row push, as in m1b34.
- **The edge gets it right:** its ls-refs takes HEAD from the same `settings` row and falls back to the shard when the
  row is missing (`workers/edge/src/lib.rs`, `ls_refs`). GHES's own reads take a missing row as `main`
  (`crates/git/src/transfer.rs`, `defaults`).

This is a gap in G26, not test residue: every repository made without REST create (imports, `B034_CREATE=owner`)
has no row until its first settings change. The fix belongs in the shard path: `ranges` should answer the default
`refs/heads/main` when there is no row, as `defaults` does. Proposed, not built (it changes G26's decision record).

### The hostile phase

From 600 s the monorepo's clone probes waited 120 s (53 of 71) and a human push timed out at 200 s (5 of 198). The
shard side (Workers Logs, `raw/attr-mt3.txt`):
- **Site a.** The rehydrates from 701 s to 860 s (a) and 890 s (b) were the pool shards `git#0#pool:1` and
  `git#1#pool:1` warming new disks, not the first shards restarting (corrected 2026-11-19, below). Site a's first shard
  answered 45 upload-packs 503 after 600 s, within its 15 s, behind catch-ups that waited on validates and merges, so
  the edge walked a → b → c.
- **Site c (corrected 2026-11-19).** Bucket c holds no `compose/` files by design: c is not one of the monorepo's
  warm sites (`warm_sites`: a, b), and it composed 30 misses before 600 s from a's and b's chains. What stopped it was
  a stalled stock reader: from about 20:36Z one stock `pack-objects` sat at 30.42 CPU-s, unchanged in every
  `shardprocs` sample until the 20:51 sweep, holding the network's lock shared. Its caller had stopped reading but kept
  the connection, so the write blocked rather than failed. No catch-up stored on c's monorepo object after 672.9 s,
  and every later upload-pack there answered 503 `warming` (64 after 600 s). Site b's monorepo object (`6d812607`) did
  the same with a stalled reader at 39.28 CPU-s from about 20:36Z: last catch-up stored at 596 s, 67 503s after 600 s.
  Fixed by a deadline on each write to the caller (`decisions/DECISIONS.md`, "Writes to an upload-pack's caller have a
  deadline"; `TestStalledCloneReleasesTheLock`).
- So the hostile-phase clone tail is: site a rehydrating, and sites b and c each held by one stalled stock read. The
  deadline worked as designed (each 503 on time) but there was no site left to answer.

**The exit 137s (corrected 2026-11-19).** The containers API reports only `state: failed`, `exit_code: 137` and
`updated_at` for an instance, no stop reason. All 28 graft-m1 shard instances of the row ended 137, and every end time
matches a teardown in Workers Logs: the pool slots' "torn down: no lease holds it" at 20:38:37 to 20:45:25Z, the row's
`/teardown` kill at 20:31:20Z (312.6 s), and `teardown.py --sweep` at 20:51:26 to 20:51:51Z and 21:05:37Z. So 137 here is
how a destroyed container exits (SIGKILL), not an out-of-memory kill. What the API no longer shows is site a's monorepo
instances before 20:31:20Z (it lists only the latest instance per name), so the rehydrates at 440 s and 701 s on site
a, which the shard logged with new disks (`55094fd6`, `79ae2c91`) on pool slots 5 and 1, are pool shards
warming, not restarts of `git#0#net:1048596`. Recorded as F95 (`decisions/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`).
