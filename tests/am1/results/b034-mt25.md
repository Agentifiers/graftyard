## Why parts stop during a fold

[read from the code at `2a020ed1`; shown on mt25's timeline above]

**The short answer: the fold is a step.** It is not a separate job that a lock makes parts wait for. The builder runs
one step at a time, and a step is either a part or a base. On the 20th move the step builds a base instead of a part,
and it puts nothing until that base exists. All three things the lead asked about hold at once:

1. **The builder's single loop.** In `workers/site/src/shard.rs`, `Compose::start` runs only when no step is running
   for that object and network (the `running` flag). The step is `Compose::run`, then `ShardHost::ready_chain`, then
   `compose::builder_step`, then `ShardHost::compose_step`. That last call is one `POST /compose?net=N&start=1` to the
   container, and the shard awaits its answer before it puts any file or `head`. Catch-ups that end while a step runs
   start nothing new, so tip moves pile up behind it.
2. **The container's lock.** In `containers/git/server/compose.go`, `handleCompose` takes `composeLock(net)` for the
   whole step ("One build or install of a network's chain at a time").
3. **The head waits on the base.** In `handleCompose`, `base := !have || len(h.Parts)+1 >= foldEvery`. When it is
   true, the step runs `rev-list --objects <tree>` and `packTo` (`pack-objects --window=0 --threads=1` through
   `m.gitLow`, nice 19) over every tree and blob of the tip, about 258 MB for the mono. Only after that does it write
   `head.json` and answer. No part for that move is built at all.

On mt25 the step that started at about −122 s was that fold. It had not finished at −2 s, after 14.7 CPU-s at client
CPU 1.0, so no part was put from the last part at 23:21 to the kill.

**Why the restarted home builds the base before any part.** `compose::builder_step` installs the buckets' chain on a
container that holds none, then calls `step`. The installed chain has 19 parts, so the container's rule makes that
first step the fold again. The killed build left nothing to resume: the new container's disk was fresh (`72d4bf`), and
the build wrote only under `pack/tmp-build`. So the restart pays the fold in full before the first part.

**Could it publish parts against the old base first?** Yes, if the fold is not the step. A chain may hold more than 19
parts. The part naming (`part-<from>-<to>`), `ChainHead::packs`, the edge's checks and the shard's puts take any
length. The limits are costs, not rules:
- `HEAD_MAX` (2,048 bytes) fits about 20 packs' records (`head-etags`). Past that the builder puts the chain alone, and
  the edge checks every pack, 4 at a time within `CHAIN_CHECK_WITHIN`, as before `head-etags`.
- Each extra pack is one more 12-byte check and one more body opened in turn on a composed hit.

## The plan (not built; for the lead's approval)

**Fold aside.** The container builds the fold base beside the steps, not as one of them. Steps keep building parts on
the old chain until the base exists, and the next step after that swaps the chain onto it.

1. **The trigger.** A step that leaves the chain at `foldStart` parts or more starts one background fold for the
   network, if none is running. `foldStart` is 12 [guess]. That leaves room for 8 moves before 20 packs, about 80 s at
   mt25's pace of one part about every 10 s, against mt24's 45 s fold after its rehydrate. The fold is built from the chain tip at that moment, with
   the same `rev-list` and `packTo` at nice 19 as today, and with its own per-network lock, not `composeLock`.
2. **Its files.** The fold writes under `compose/<net>/fold/`, not `objects/pack/`. `dropUnnamed` deletes every chain
   pack the head does not name, so a fold built in `objects/pack/` would be deleted by the next part's step.
3. **The swap.** The step that finds a finished fold whose tip `T` is still in its chain (the base or a part) builds the
   new head as today's fold plus one part. The base is `base-T`, moved in from `fold/`. It is followed by one part from
   `T` to the new tip, computed by `newObjects` and `missingFromChain` against the new base, not the old chain. The old
   chain's parts after `T` are not kept: they left out objects the old base held, which `base-T` may not hold. A fold
   whose tip is no longer in the chain, because an install or a far tip (`composeDiffMax`) replaced it, is deleted.
4. **The ceiling.** A step at `partsMax` parts, 40 [guess], folds inline as today. That keeps the chain finite if the
   background fold keeps failing. Between 20 and 40 parts the head drops its records past `HEAD_MAX`, and the edge
   checks every pack.
5. **After a restart.** `builder_step` is unchanged. It installs the buckets' chain, and its first step is a part,
   because the fold is no longer a step below `partsMax`. That step starts the fold beside it. So a killed fold is
   redone after parts resume, not first. It is not resumed: a new container's disk is empty.

**Writers.** None is added. The container builds the base, as today, and only the home's first shard (the builder)
puts files and `head` to `compose/<net>/`, through `ready_chain`, as today. The swap's head is put like today's fold
head: its files first, then `head`, then the old base's files are deleted (`ready_chain`, `old.chain.base !=
head.base`). C stays rejected.

**What ships.** The container only (`containers/git/server/compose.go`): a new shard image on a site day, with no edge
day and no site-script logic change. The wire is unchanged.

**What it costs** [inference]. Folds start at 12 parts, not 20, so about 1.5 times as many base builds and base puts
(258 MB to each warm bucket). Under load, as on mt25 (a fold over 120 s at about 12% of a vCPU), the chain will pass 20
packs. Its hits then check every pack, which `head-etags` was built to avoid (mt15: `chain_open` p50 469 to 695 ms at
11 to 20 checks). The next row reads that: `server-timing`'s `chain_head` against `chain_chk`, and hits p99.

**Tests and plants** (plants from 164):
- A Go test that fails on main: with a chain at `foldStart` parts and the fold held (a test hook), steps keep answering
  parts within their usual time, and the chain grows; once the fold is released, the next step answers `base-T` plus
  one part, and a depth-1 clone of the new tip through `/upload-pack` composes and passes `git fsck`. On main the step
  at the 20th move blocks on the base.
- A Go test of the restart: install a 19-part chain on an empty container, then step. It answers a part. On main it
  answers a base.
- Plants:
  - 164: the fold runs inline again (the step waits for it);
  - 165: the swap keeps the old chain's parts after `T`;
  - 166: the fold writes into `objects/pack/` (so `dropUnnamed` deletes it);
  - 167: the swap uses a fold whose tip is not in the chain;
  - 168: no ceiling at `partsMax`.

**What it changes in DECISIONS.** A new entry, `fold-aside`, in the shape of the others:
- decision: the steps above;
- why: mt25's timeline;
- what it beat: E (a local chain on the other site), raising `foldEvery`, and a higher nice for the fold;
- evidence: the tests and plants;
- flip: a row whose hits p99 or `chain_open` rises past `head-etags`' gain because chains run past 20 packs, or a fold
  that never lands before `partsMax` under load.

`builder-install` gains one line: its first step after an install is now a part even at 19 parts.

**What it changes in G37 and DESIGN.** G37 records mt25 and the ruling (done on this branch). Once the fix is built, it
will also record the fix, and the row that settles it:
- no gap of more than 60 s [guess] between part puts outside the home's own rehydrate;
- every clone ≥ 90% in kill and second;
- composed misses on the moved-load site at p99 under 5 s while held.

DESIGN's "Git cache shards" sentence "a part per move after its catch-up, and a fold every 20" becomes "a part per move,
and a fold built beside the parts from 12 and swapped in at the next step, inline only at 40".
